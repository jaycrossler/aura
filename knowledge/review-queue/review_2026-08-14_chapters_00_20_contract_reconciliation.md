# Chapters 0-20 Contract Reconciliation Review

## Recommendation

Keep the current manuscript order through Chapter 17. Do not force it back into the older master scene index. The prose has already established a better current sequence, but the contracts have not been reconciled to it.

Treat Chapters 15 through 20 as one revised escalation block:

1. **15, Sideways:** controlled wonder, repeatable crossing, early material questions.
2. **16, Ten Good Days:** instrumentation and transfer momentum, followed by neglect costs.
3. **17, The Eye in the Wall:** empirical anomaly becomes threat; friendship and work costs converge.
4. **18, Quiet Companion:** escape without victory, responsibility repair, bounded Aura installation, Adama anomaly.
5. **19, No Vampires:** human Will and healing become an ethical problem before they become a power system.
6. **20, The Leech:** the first intervention succeeds but creates a physical wound, an unknown absorption, and the hidden condition for the later two-cores error.

This sequence scales wonder into dread and then cost. It also preserves the Arc 1 ceiling. Jace solves problems with observation, ordered materials, instruments, and other people's expertise. He does not suddenly become a combat specialist.

## Findings from Chapters 0-17

### What is working

- The current opening on the Falcon is more coherent than the old Mars-first documentation. Helena's prologue gives the loss emotional weight before Jace learns it.
- Chapters 1 through 6 establish Jace as useful but not dominant. Plumbing, repairs, crew rounds, Learning Mode, and the Spire all reward diagnosis and persistence rather than power.
- Chapters 7 through 11 make Fortuna materially credible. Work queues, delivery access, factions, the Helena investigation, the intact-suit wound, and K-17 all plant later story functions.
- Chapters 12 and 13 give Jace and Sophia distinct discovery experiences. Jace crosses accidentally with Cerberus; Sophia brings forty prior experiments and scientific discipline.
- Chapters 14 through 17 use the same location in physical and Astral forms. Factory Four and K-17 now matter to construction, mystery, and the later battle.
- Cerberus consistently acts as a protected companion and threat detector. Chapter 17 correctly makes the new creature turn toward him, which immediately changes Jace's risk tolerance.

### Structural drift

The largest issue is not prose continuity. It is contract continuity.

The Arc 1 master scene index still assumes a sequence from before the present Chapters 15 through 17. Those chapters have already consumed or partially consumed later beats:

- X-01 now includes repeated joint crossings and the Astral name.
- X-09b's qualitative material differences begin in Chapter 15.
- X-09d's portable lab work and X-09e's Sparky transfer occur in Chapter 16.
- A-05 is divided between Chapter 14's first K-17 survey and Chapter 17's physical silt confirmation.
- X-01b setup is no longer a lighthearted request from Nick. Chapter 17 makes it a damaged-trust scene.

The scene index also points to obsolete filenames and contains gaps in its sequence numbering. Updating the index to the manuscript is safer than moving prose to match the index.

### Emotional and tension scaling

The tension curve is strong through Chapter 7, softens during settlement, and rises sharply at Chapter 17. The missing bridge was consequence.

- **Wonder:** Chapters 12 through 16 earn awe through sensory difference, repeatability, and small engineering wins.
- **Dread:** Chapter 17 changes the same geometry from interesting to predatory.
- **Personal cost:** Chapter 18 should not add a bigger creature. It should make Jace face Kim, Nick, crossing debt, and Artemis's damaged messages.
- **Moral cost:** Chapter 19 should establish privacy, consent, and no-harvest rules before the protagonists learn that Will can affect a body.
- **Physical cost:** Chapter 20 should make the first successful rescue injure Jace and leave Sophia with an unexplained internal change.

This progression avoids three weak patterns: a threat followed immediately by an easy victory, a discovery that behaves like a free upgrade, and a friend who forgives secrecy because the plot needs equipment.

### Character behavior checks

| Character | Nature to preserve | Drift to avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Jace | Diagnoses systems, protects Cerberus, uses humor under stress, accepts responsibility when forced to see the human cost | Letting him ignore Kim's consequences for another chapter, or giving him advanced combat judgment before he earns it |
| Sophia | Precise, cautious, empirically disciplined, emotionally more expressive than she first appears | Having her casually leave the Victoria after Chapter 17, ignore consent, or absorb an orb without a conscious decision |
| Nick | Perceptive, technically rigorous, loyal, committed to honest open systems | Having him accept a weak cover story, forget his anger, or install Aura without rollback and boundaries |
| Kim | Better than Jace at the actual delivery job and responsible for people inside the system | Reducing her to someone who merely blocks Jace's clever ideas |
| Lanchee | Quiet, exact, and medically responsible | Allowing an unexplained patient experiment or a supernatural wound without consent, records, and consequences |
| Cerberus | Protected companion, warning sense, emotional anchor | Using him as expendable proof that a creature is dangerous |
