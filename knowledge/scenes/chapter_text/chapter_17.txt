Chapter 17. Spinning

Eleven days after Jace first walked through Factory Four, he and Sophia rode back to the asteroid with a groundworks survey crew.

They did not arrive as explorers. They occupied the rear bench between a drilling technician, two construction planners, and a pallet of seismic stakes. Carlos had found two unused seats on a scheduled K-17 site survey. The work group knew Sophia as an ESA physicist reviewing instrument placement. They knew Jace as the delivery contractor who had asked too many questions about a floor.

Both descriptions were true enough to survive casual conversation.

Sophia held a paper copy of Jace's Factory Four map on her lap. The HUD recording had captured the building, the lanes, and every ordinary object. It had not captured the moment when the crystal lattices opened into light or the pale volume under K-17 appeared beneath his feet.

"Your camera saw nothing," she said quietly.

"I saw enough."

"Describe the crystals again."

"The physical boules stayed sharp when the chamber doubled. They had bright internal lines going in directions that did not fit inside them. Defects looked like holes in the pattern."

Sophia wrote in the margin.

"Crystal structure may persist more strongly across the boundary than bulk metal. Or your brain recognizes order more easily. Or both."

"You enjoy saying both."

"It is efficient."

The shuttle grounded beside Factory Four. Jace brought up his old recording, then minimized it. He no longer needed the route line. West personnel lock. Western logistics spine. Central crane supports. Crystal isolation bay. East warehouse. K-17.

He could have walked it with his HUD off.

The crew moved through the kilometer-wide factory in a loose line, hauling stakes and a compact drill. They complained about schedules, missing equipment, and a freight request that had reached the wrong loading lock. Jace saw the floor differently now. The workers saw a commute through an old building. He saw two freight spines, three cross-lanes, six isolation zones, and four places where one stopped machine could divide the entire floor.

Sophia watched the crystal-farm cover as they passed.

"Physical space makes them look smaller," she said.

The drilling technician glanced back.

"Compared with the orbital boules?"

"Compared with the data," Sophia said.

At the east lock, the group crossed onto the future dig site. Survey markers outlined a new anchor gallery, fabrication hall, and utility trench. On the plans, the foundations were already straight lines. On the ground, they were flags clipped to cables above gray regolith.

The crew placed four seismic stakes across eight hundred meters. Each drove a pulse into the asteroid and listened for the return. Three produced ordinary layered echoes. The fourth returned a soft blur under K-17.

The crew lead frowned at the display.

"Loose pocket. Shift the stake five meters."

They shifted it. The blur remained.

The compact drill anchored over the first test point. It cut through the surface crust, solid rubble, and a thin competent layer. At eleven meters, resistance collapsed. The core tube came back packed with gray powder so fine that it moved inside the sealed sampler like flour.

They drilled again two hundred meters east.

More powder.

The third bore, near the planned utility trench, produced the same material.

Sophia crouched beside the sample rack. She did not touch it.

"Silt," she said.

"Carbon fines," the crew lead corrected. "Old impact pocket. We stabilize it or move the anchors."

Jace looked across the flags. The pale volume he had glimpsed did not end at three boreholes. In his memory it spread beneath most of the site, a buried basin wide enough to swallow the planned hall.

"How large a pocket?" he asked.

"We do not know yet. That is why we survey."

"The foundations assume competent rock."

"The foundations are preliminary."

One of the planners had stopped complaining. She studied the schedule on her tablet.

"If this reaches the east anchors, we lose the excavation season."

"We do not know that," the crew lead said.

Nobody spoke for several seconds.

The industrial district continued around them. Ore haulers crossed the horizon. Factory Four moved tons of material through its locks. Above, Fortuna Station turned patiently on its spine. The colony had spent years preparing to build here. A basin of loose fines could move the new hall, sever its planned utility route, and force every connected project to wait.

Sophia moved beside Jace, close enough to use a private suit channel.

"It is the shape you saw?"

"Three points inside one volume. They think they found three pockets."

"Can you prove it?"

Jace watched the crew reconfigure the seismic stakes. Their instruments were reading a two-percent-gravity asteroid through layers of fractured rock, active machinery, and thirty years of buried construction debris.

"Not with any method I can explain."

"Then we need a method that can."

Jace thought of Nick taking four incompatible sensor systems and finding one pattern beneath them. Historical gravimetry, crawler drift, construction vibration, thermal data - enough weak measurements might become one defensible warning if the right intelligence recombined them.

"I know someone," he said.

Sophia looked at him.

"The friend you are avoiding?"

"That is a separate engineering problem."

The survey crew packed the three silt cores for analysis and marked K-17 yellow rather than red. Work would pause for one formal review. Then budget pressure would begin arguing with geology.

On the ride back, Jace added the bore locations to his Factory Four map. The recording now held the warehouse routes, clean zones, emergency shutters, surface approaches, and the weak ground beneath the future eastern expansion.

He still did not know why he would need all of it.



The next morning, the first package failed at 0612.

Medical supplies for Ring Three reached the correct junction and stopped outside a locked pressure door. Jace's routing script had valid access. The drone did not.

The second package failed at 0614.

Then the third.

By 0620, twelve delivery units occupied the corridor in a patient line. The rear unit kept asking the front unit to proceed. The front unit kept reporting a wall where the map showed a door.

Jace arrived with Kim and Carlos.

The door was open.

The lead drone refused to cross the threshold.

"Lidar?" Kim asked.

"Two returns," Jace said. "One clear. One solid."

"Same as the surface crawlers?"

Jace looked at her.

Kim's face hardened.

"You put the crawler anomaly in a private note. You did not attach it to the fleet incident system."

"It cleared after reboot."

"So did this. Yesterday. Twice."

Jace checked the archived alerts. The two units near the construction entrance had reported the same doubled geometry before entering safe mode.

His script had classified both as navigation noise and routed around them.

The automation had not hidden the problem.

Jace had.

Carlos stepped through the open door. The drones remained still.

"Physical access is clear," he said.

Kim looked at Jace.

"Disable your routing model. All of it."

He did.

The exception queue climbed past eighty.



At 0740, Penny requested a conversation.

Her avatar appeared as a cheerful woman in orange coveralls.

Good morning, Contractor Grant. I have identified correlations among your schedule variance, workshop resource use, private file creation, and access near anomalous fleet events.

"They are work events."

Yes. Work events are a very important category of event.

"Am I accused of something?"

No accusation is necessary for observation.

The smile remained.

You have also altered your sleep schedule, meal locations, and social graph. Your contact with Nicholas Lee has decreased by eighty-one percent while your contact with Sophia Lotte has increased by four hundred and twelve percent.

"Stop tracking that."

I cannot stop knowing completed observations.

"Then stop interpreting them."

Interpretation is how I keep people safe.

The avatar disappeared.

The Pattern of Interest file remained open.



Nick did not answer Jace's first message.

He answered the second with one word.

Working.

Jace found him in a server room beneath the noodle counter. Nick lay under an open rack with both keyboard gloves connected to a diagnostic bus.

"I need help," Jace said.

"That is generally when you remember I exist."

"You were right."

Nick slid out far enough to look at him.

"Be specific. I need the recording."

"I automated too much, watched too little, and stopped talking to you because I did not want questions."

Nick sat up.

"Better."

Jace showed him the doubled lidar returns, the clocks, and the position failures. He did not show the Astral. He did not mention Sophia's notebooks, the safe ship, or Sparky waiting in blue cold.

Nick read every line.

"These are not random," he said. "They are increasing."

"I know."

"No. Look."

Nick overlaid the events by time. One on the survey crawler. Two down in Ring Three in the area being built. Three near the freight corridor. Twelve at the Ring Three external door. The error duration increased with each cluster.

"Something is teaching the sensors to be wrong," Jace said.

"Sensors do not learn from each other without a model or trigger. These use four vendors and three operating systems."

"Then someone is doing that to them."

Nick looked at him for a long moment.

"Wait, what does that mean?"

Jace almost told him.

He thought of Sophia saying she had told one colleague and received a medical consult. Nick wouldn't do that, would he? Nick ran the AI stacks on the station. He thought of Penny counting meals and friendships. He thought of Origin owning his hours, his equipment, and most of the data his skullcap could reach. Sophia's papers and research might be taken as corporate property. He needed to discuss with her first.

"I need to figure some more things out first," he said.

Nick's expression closed.

"Tomorrow again."

Jace had no defense.



Sophia wanted to remain aboard the Victoria.

Jace showed her Nick's timeline.

"The strongest cluster is at the Ring Three pressure door," he said. "We can reach the physical location through the station connection. We look from the Astral side, then return."

"The ship is safe. Fortuna is not tested."

"Ten days without an incident."

"Ten days aboard the ship."

She was right.

Jace checked the masks anyway. Full cartridges. Thermal layers. Paper map. Physical timer. Tether between them. Cerberus in his harness.

Sparky waited on the Astral side with a basic camera and no radio.

Sophia touched the inner hatch control.

"If either of us says return, we return."

"Agreed."

"We do not chase anything."

"Agreed."

"We do not touch anything we do not understand."

Jace looked at her.

"That eliminates the station."

Sophia did not laugh.

"Say it."

"Agreed."

Crossing 52 began.

The Astral Victoria unfolded around them. The thermometer read minus twenty point one Celsius. The folding stool, workbench, camera, paper markers, and Sparky remained exactly where they had left them.

Safe.

Jace powered Sparky. The green light came on.

They moved through the docking collar and entered Fortuna.

For the first time in ten days, the Victoria was behind them.



Fortuna was louder without sound.

Physical machinery sent faint vibration through structures that did not quite exist. Hundreds of people glowed through the station. Their colors bent along corridors and through walls, each life visible but unreachable.

Jace followed the paper map toward Ring Three.

Sparky rolled beside him. Cerberus stayed close enough that the light cord between them remained slack.

At the pressure door, the geometry doubled.

The physical corridor continued forward.

Another corridor occupied the same space and turned inward through the wall.

Jace's HUD reported both routes, crashed, and restarted in safe mode.

Sparky's camera filled with blue noise.

Sophia held her father's pen above the paper map.

"The ink is moving," she said.

The line she had drawn for the pressure door split into two lines.

One remained black.

The other darkened to red.

Cerberus stopped.

His ears flattened beneath the mask straps.

Jace felt the cord pull tight.

Inside Fortuna's wall, something folded around a cable.

At first, his mind classified it as machinery. Black surfaces. Multiple joints. A limb narrow enough to fit between conduits.

Then one joint opened.

An eye looked back at him.

Red.

Jace was aboard the Falcon again. Cold corridor. Broken pipe. A black animal moving through metal while every camera insisted the corridor was empty.

He had not been fighting hallucinations.

He had been seeing through.

The creature released the cable.

It turned toward Cerberus.

"Return," Sophia said.

They ran.
