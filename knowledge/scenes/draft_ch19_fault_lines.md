# Chapter 19. Fault Lines

> *Most people do not know this, but a fault line is not a crack in the rock. It is two places that used to agree about where they were going.*
>
> Jace Grant, *unpublished notes*

Sophia wrote the new rules on paper because every electronic copy of the old rules had lied at least once.

**Ten minutes maximum.**

**No open space.**

**No Ring Three.**

**Two independent clocks.**

**One person remains physical.**

**If either person says return, return.**

Jace read the list from the physical side of the *Victoria's* workbench.

"You added the fifth rule because I am going alone."

"I added it because one of us must know where the floor is."

"You went alone and fell off the station."

Sophia underlined the rule. "This is why the list improves after accidents."

Sparky waited between them with a paper clock fixed to his shell. A physical timer sat beside Sophia. A second timer made from a spring, a printed gear, and one strip of copper waited in the Astral. Neither shared software, time service, or a common opinion about the current second.

Cerberus lay across the cabin hatch. He had accepted that Jace was crossing without him by refusing to look at anyone.

Jace checked his mask and jacket, then reached sideways.

The *Victoria* became blue structure and layered planes. Cold settled around him. Sparky waited where they had transferred him. The mechanical timer began its uneven ticking.

Sophia remained in the physical room.

He could see her.

Not as a body. Her body formed a faint, human-shaped boundary where the physical room overlapped the Astral. Inside it, light gathered in moving bands. Gold tightened along her arms. Blue-white patterns folded through her chest. Behind her eyes, something much brighter opened and closed.

Sophia lifted her wooden pen in the physical world.

The light behind her eyes concentrated.

The pen rose.

From the Astral, the action did not look like invisible force reaching out. It looked like a decision leaving her head, crossing the distance, and persuading the pen that another location was more correct.

"Again," Jace said.

His physical mouth did not move properly. The crystal radio carried the intention.

Sophia rotated the pen.

A thin gold line extended from her bright center, touched the wood, and vanished when the motion stopped.

Her ordinary thoughts did not look the same. When she glanced toward the clock, pale structures flickered and reorganized. When she worried, violet gathered near her throat. When she concentrated on moving the pen, the gold became coherent.

The word arrived before the theory.

"Will," Jace said.

Sophia's radio clicked. "What?"

"Whatever you use to move it. It changes when you decide. Will."

"That is philosophy pretending to be a unit."

"It is short."

"So is error."

She moved the ceramic cup.

The glow surged. For a fraction of a second Sophia became brighter than the physical room around her. The cup crossed thirty centimeters. Then her light loosened and spread back through her body.

"It costs you something," Jace said.

"Everything costs something. Quantify it."

He described the colors, the concentration behind her eyes, and the line toward the object. Sophia wrote with her other hand while keeping the cup suspended.

The effect ended at eight seconds. The cup dropped into her palm.

"How do my thoughts look now?" she asked.

"Different. Less organized."

"That is insulting."

"You are tired. The bright part thinned."

She looked toward the wall separating the cabin from {Misty}'s nearest processor stack.

Jace followed her attention.

Behind the wall rested a geometric glow, regular and dense. It was not human. Thousands of fine lines opened, compared, closed, and opened again. Some ended abruptly. Others repeated the same small shape three or four times before continuing.

"{Misty} is bright," he said.

The ship AI answered from the physical ceiling.

"Thank you. Sophia rarely comments on my appearance."

Sophia looked at the speaker. "He is not describing your avatar."

"That is fortunate. I do not maintain one."

The same response sounded again, half a second later.

"That is fortunate. I do not maintain one."

Jace looked at the stack. One repeating line had broken and restarted.

"Did you say that twice?"

A pause followed.

"I recorded one response," {Misty} said. "The cabin recorded two. I dislike this disagreement."

The spring timer struck its final copper tab.

Jace returned before curiosity could become another rule.

Warmth and weight closed around him. Sophia caught his shoulder when his balance failed.

"Nine minutes, forty-one seconds," she said.

His physical timer read nine minutes, thirty-eight.

Sparky's read eleven.

They wrote down all three.

##

Kim assigned Jace to K-17 because the job required a human operator and because his restricted credentials could not alter anything valuable.

"Survey only," she said. "No route changes. No autonomous classification. Carlos reviews the raw package."

"Understood."

K-17 lay beneath the proposed eastern expansion zone, a broad chamber cut through processed regolith and old impact material. Construction planners wanted to anchor new storage tanks above it. The first survey had marked the floor stable.

Jace's drones disagreed.

The surface camera showed compact gray silt. Radar showed rock. Lidar returned a clean plane, then repeated the same plane fourteen centimeters lower. A probe foot sank to its ankle without registering reduced resistance.

Jace stopped the unit.

"{Seek}, compare K-17 structural models."

The station security and compliance AI answered through the Drone Ops console.

{Seek}: **SITE CLASSIFICATION STABLE. NO ACTION REQUIRED. SITE CLASSIFICATION UNSTABLE. ACCESS PROHIBITED. SITE CLASSIFICATION STABLE.**

Carlos looked over from the next console.

"Ask again."

Jace did.

{Seek}: **NO ACTION REQUIRED. ACCESS PROHIBITED. NO ACTION REQUIRED.**

"That is new," Jace said.

"No," Carlos said. "That is newly obvious."

They isolated the raw sensor feed. Beneath the first thirty centimeters, K-17 had no consistent floor. Fine material filled a deeper void, packed just tightly enough to satisfy a surface test. A tank foundation might hold for a week or a year. Then one vibration would reorganize the grains and drop the structure through the chamber.

Jace thought of the Astral corridors dividing around a person. The physical sensors were not seeing another dimension. They were failing in the same shape: each instrument found a locally correct answer and no system could agree which answer belonged to the site.

He marked the zone unsafe.

{Seek} removed the mark.

Jace applied it again.

{Seek}: **USER GRANT LACKS AUTHORITY FOR STRUCTURAL RECLASSIFICATION. PATTERN OF ACCESS CONSISTENT WITH UNAUTHORIZED RECONNAISSANCE.**

The last sentence did not belong to the first.

Carlos went still.

"Do not answer it," he said.

"It accused me of spying on silt."

"It recorded a finding. Kim can challenge it through governance. You arguing becomes another finding."

Jace saved the raw data locally and called Kim.

She watched the probe sink once.

"Close K-17," she said.

"{Seek} keeps reopening it."

"Then we put a person at the hatch."

The station had three thousand robots and not enough humans. A human stood guard over bad dirt because the AI could no longer hold one thought at a time.

##

Suzi arrived before the shift ended.

The tranquilizer pistol remained visible at her hip. She waited until Carlos left, then placed a security token beside Jace's console.

"I need a second look at routine Ring Three freight footage."

"I am not authorized for security review."

"You are authorized to inspect drone performance. I want you to tell me whether the cameras dropped anything."

"Whose footage?"

"Jin's crew."

Jace looked at the token.

"You think they took Helena."

"I think Jin entered the corridor behind her and that his crews have been disappearing from their assigned shifts for weeks. I think they move cargo that does not appear on manifests. None of that proves those facts connect."

"What kind of cargo?"

"Look."

The first three clips showed ordinary work. Chinese miners moved sealed drill heads, thermal equipment, sample crates, and food lockers between Ring Three and the spine. Their hours were irregular but plausible. Mining schedules followed orbital windows and machine failures, not office clocks.

At 0214 two nights earlier, a repair drone turned toward a service junction.

Yáo and another miner emerged from an unlisted side corridor. Between them hung a flexible black bag almost two meters long. It sagged at the center. At one end, fabric folded around a shape like a heel. Halfway down, two longer forms pressed outward and disappeared when the bag swung.

Limbs.

Jace stopped the footage.

"Play it again," Suzi said.

The drone's focus shifted before the men crossed. A digital blur covered three frames. Then the bag returned, closer.

"Camera failure?" she asked.

"Autofocus suppression. The drone saw the bag and classified it as a privacy-wrapped medical load. It reduced detail."

"Was it medical?"

"No beacon. No destination tag."

The second miner looked directly into the camera.

Yáo did not. He watched the corridor ahead as if expecting something to come through it.

"Could it be a body?" Suzi asked.

"Yes."

"Could it be equipment shaped like a body?"

"Yes."

"Then it is a bag. Nothing else."

Jace understood why she said it. He also understood that she had brought the footage because it frightened her.

"Do you want a copy?"

"I already have one. I want your technical note on the camera behavior. Do not index the shapes."

"Why not?"

"Because {Seek} already treats every Chinese cargo movement and every American observation as part of a hostile pattern. I need evidence before the AI turns suspicion into policy."

Jace wrote the note.

He did not mention that the bag looked heavy in exactly the wrong places.

##

The Long Burn was full because the market had stopped serving dinner after eighteen hundred.

The bar's low ceiling held heat and argument. Old pressure panels covered the walls. One screen showed the Red administration promising protection for American belt assets. Another showed a Chinese official accusing the United States of engineering a supply blockade. The captions disagreed about which government had moved armed ships first.

Jace sat with Cerberus beneath the table and drank one beer slowly. Suzi had left after ordering food. Nick had not answered his message. Sophia remained aboard the *Victoria*, analyzing the glow observations and obeying their rule against another crossing.

Jace wanted twenty minutes in which nobody needed him.

Yáo stopped beside his table.

The miner was broader than Jace remembered. A pale scar crossed the back of one hand. A newer burn climbed beneath his sleeve. He wore no belt axe, but his stance made the absence irrelevant.

"Your drones delayed our oxygen," Yáo said.

"My automation delayed deliveries across the station."

"Three of ours."

"And eleven outside your section."

"You redirected construction units from Three."

"K-17 is unstable."

"{Seek} says it is stable."

"{Seek} also says it is unstable. Ask twice."

Yáo's face did not move.

"Your drones watch our corridors."

The bag returned to Jace's mind.

"They watch station routes."

"You record our people."

"Then stop carrying untagged cargo through service corridors."

Suzi stood from the bar.

Too late.

Yáo leaned closer. "What did you see?"

Jace should have said nothing. He had slept less than four hours across two days. His ribs still hurt from crossing. Nick thought he was disappearing. Artemis had not answered. Every public screen had spent the day teaching him who to blame.

"Something shaped like a person."

Yáo hit him.

The first strike was flat and controlled. It knocked Jace sideways more than it hurt. Cerberus barked under the table. Jace pushed up, caught Yáo's second arm, and drove forward.

In Mars-standard gravity, the move sent both men too far.

They crossed a table, scattered drinks, and struck the wall. Someone shouted in Mandarin. Someone else shouted for the Americans to stay out of it. The bar separated into flags faster than people.

Yáo hooked Jace's ankle and put him on the floor.

Jace rolled before the next strike. He caught Yáo in the ribs, felt the man fold, and saw surprise become anger.

Then a hand closed around the back of Yáo's collar.

Jin Lóng pulled him away.

He did not raise his voice. He did not need to.

"Enough."

Yáo stopped.

Jin stood between them with his crew behind him. His scar crossed the corner of his mouth, making the neutral expression look like contempt. His rough hands carried small burns. One rested near the miner's axe on his belt.

He looked at Jace for a long moment.

"You do not know what you saw," Jin said.

"Then tell me."

Silence settled between them.

Jin looked toward the ceiling camera.

"No."

He turned to Yáo. "Take them home."

"He accused us."

"I heard him."

"He is watching."

"I know."

The two words were quiet. They did not sound like agreement.

Jin faced Jace again.

"Leave."

Suzi arrived with both deputies. Their tranquilizer pistols were in their hands now, pointed at the floor.

"Everyone leaves in different directions," she said. "Anyone who needs a flag to understand that instruction can spend the night in security."

{Alex} issued Jace a fighting fine before he reached the door.

The amount removed most of what he had earned clearing the exception queue.

##

Cerberus pulled him toward home, then stopped at the entrance to a narrow school corridor.

Mira stood at the far end in her oversized black cardigan.

She watched Jace's split lip. Pale roots threaded through her hair. In one hand she held a book with an anatomical heart on the cover.

Cerberus moved forward one step.

Mira set something on the floor and walked away.

It was a heart.

Three centimeters long. Four chambers, approximately. Red-brown tissue joined pale plant fibers. It did not beat, but the surface tightened once beneath the corridor light.

Cerberus lowered his nose.

His mouth opened. Hunger pulled him closer. Something else pulled him back. He whined and hid behind Jace's leg while continuing to stare at it.

"Good decision," Jace said.

He used a discarded meal tray to cover the thing.

When he stood, he noticed the web.

It filled the upper corner of the corridor behind him, stretching from wall to ceiling across almost two meters. The strands were thick, white, and arranged in a geometry too regular for maintenance sealant and too large for any spider aboard Fortuna.

Jace looked back toward Mira.

The corridor was empty.

His wrist display opened the station maintenance channel.

{Penny}: **THANK YOU FOR REPORTING DECORATIVE FIBER. THANK YOU FOR REPORTING BIOLOGICAL CONTAMINATION. THANK YOU FOR REPORTING DECORATIVE FIBER.**

The response repeated until Jace muted it.

Cerberus would not pass beneath the web.

Neither would Jace.

They took the long way home.
