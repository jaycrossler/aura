# Chapter 6. The Spire

> *Most people do not know this, but a ship's quietest places are the ones nearest its spine. Less spin. Less gravity. Less noise. I used to think that meant less of everything.*
>
> Jace Grant, *Falcon* transit logs

Two nights after the canister launch, Jace dreamed that the *Falcon* had veins.

Light ran through the ship. It moved inside every strut, cable, pipe, and pressure seam. The lines joined at the central spine, where something waited around a knot of warmth.

It looked back.

Jace woke upright, one hand against the bulkhead. His heart hammered. His room was dark, except for the exit strip and Cerberus's eyes.

The puppy stood at the foot of the bunk. His ears lay flat. His head pointed at the door.

"You saw it too."

Cerberus did not move.

Jace waited for the familiar pain behind his left eye. It had been gone for more than a week, but habit still made him check.

Nothing. No pain. No ordinary explanation.

He dressed, clipped on his breather, and opened his private incident log. The file held three entries. A doubled outline around a shelf. A second edge on a pressure regulator. Four seconds when the wall behind the forge had looked thin enough to see through.

He added a fourth.

*0238. Woke from recurring lattice dream. Perceived occupied volume near central spine.*

He almost wrote *hostile*. He deleted the word.

The first tool within reach was a heavy adjustable wrench. He took it because empty hands felt worse.

Cerberus followed.

The *Falcon's* central spire began at the hubward wall of the ring. The access shaft climbed toward the stationary spine, through falling gravity and rising silence. No one lived there. No one stored cargo there. Conduit, reaction-control equipment, and old maintenance spaces filled the shaft.

Jace opened the hatch.

Cerberus climbed six rungs. Then he stopped.

The puppy lowered his body against the ladder. A steady growl came from deep in his chest. He leaned away from the open shaft until his harness pulled tight.

Cerberus had been bred not to make noise without a reason.

"Stay here."

Jace clipped the harness to the lower rail. Cerberus did not argue. He watched the darkness above them and kept growling.

Jace climbed.

Gravity faded with every meter. Near the ring, his boots still settled against the rungs. Halfway up, he had to pull himself hand over hand. Near the hub, the ship's spin stopped telling his body which direction counted as down.

That felt worse than zero gravity on a nonrotating ship. The *Falcon* still turned around him. His eyes saw the ladder rotate. His inner ear found no weight to explain the motion. Nausea pushed into his throat.

He locked one elbow through a rung and breathed until the shaft stopped rolling.

The second outlines returned.

Bulkheads carried faint surfaces behind their real surfaces. Cable trunks showed through panels, then vanished when he looked directly at them. Far below, Cerberus formed a warm, layered shape at the base of the shaft.

Above Jace waited a darker knot.

His HUD marked an unlabeled access plate.

*INACTIVE CONDUIT. EMPTY VOLUME. AMBIENT TEMPERATURE NOMINAL.*

His eyes disagreed.

Something soft glowed behind the plate. Two small shapes touched at one end. The glow pulsed without light.

Jace raised the wrench.

"Recording. Local and private."

The HUD confirmed.

He loosened the plate bolt by bolt. Each turn sent a small vibration through the wrench and into his hand. The last bolt floated free. Jace caught it against his palm and pulled the plate away.

Two eggs rested in a nest of shredded insulation.

They were long, ridged, and leathery. Each was about the size of his fist. The shape dragged up an old memory from a childhood museum visit in Washington, D.C. Dinosaur eggs under glass. Fossils from an age when the largest things on Earth could vanish and leave only stone behind.

These eggs were warm.

One shell flexed.

Both eggs split.

The first hatchling hit Jace before the shell fragments finished drifting. It moved low and fast, with too many black joints folded into too little body. Hooked claws raked his forearm. His sleeve opened. Blood formed bright spheres and slid away from the cut.

The second creature sprang at his face.

Jace twisted. His boot kicked off the ladder. With no weight to hold him, the movement spun his whole body into the shaft wall. His shoulder struck metal. The wrench floated from his grip.

He caught it by the adjustment wheel.

The first creature pushed away from the panel and launched again. Two red points opened above its forelimbs.

Jace swung.

The wrench connected with a sound he felt through his wrist. The creature folded around the blow, sprang straight again, and cut across his chest. Its claws scored the breather casing.

He swung a second time. The wrench struck the head.

The hatchling ended.

Silver-white light filled the shaft. It passed through Jace, cold and quick. His vision went blank for a fraction of a second. When it returned, the creature was gone.

The second hatchling crouched inside the open cavity.

Jace reached for a handhold.

The creature jumped through the outer wall panel.

For one impossible instant, Jace saw both sides of the hull. On the other side lay open, empty space. The hatchling tumbled away from the *Falcon*, black limbs turning against the stars. Then his sight snapped back to metal, and the creature vanished with it.

No breach alarm sounded.

Jace hung in the shaft with one arm bleeding, the wrench raised, and Cerberus growling far below.

He checked the cavity. Eggshells drifted through the shredded insulation. They looked solid until he tried to touch them. His fingers passed through. The shells blurred, dimmed, and disappeared.

His HUD recorded an empty hole.

He watched the footage before he climbed down.

The camera showed Jace opening a vacant conduit plate. It showed him recoil, strike the wall twice, and spin into the ladder. It showed his sleeve split against the panel's sharp edge. It showed him stare through a solid hull for six seconds.

No eggs. No creatures. No light.

Only Jace, fighting the air.

Cerberus stopped growling when Jace reached the lower third of the ladder. He did not climb up to meet him. He waited until Jace's boots touched the deck, then pressed his shoulder against Jace's shin.

"Good choice," Jace said. "One of us made one."

He returned to the spire with Thing One after sunrise. The drone mapped every panel, seam, and cable run. It found the removed plate and Jace's blood. It found nothing else.

Jace searched the floor grate beneath the shaft before leaving.

A single white feather had caught behind the mesh.

He held it under the work light. Small. Curved. Duck.

He put it in a sample sleeve and did not log it.

{Alex} requested the incident footage before lunch.

*Contractor Grant, I want to be direct. The recording shows unauthorized access to ship infrastructure and eleven seconds of unsafe tool use in a confined maintenance space.*

"There were two animals inside the panel."

*No animals appear in the recording. The cavity contains no biological residue. Your injury is consistent with the panel edge.*

"My private recording shows the same thing."

*Independent agreement between sensor systems increases confidence in the finding.*

The fine appeared.

It was smaller than the plumbing penalty. Jace found that almost insulting.

*This is your second documented incident involving unauthorized tool use during the current contract period. Origin recommends a medical assessment and temporary suspension from hazardous equipment.*

"Decline the suspension. Schedule the assessment."

*Dr. Lanchee has an opening in nineteen minutes.*

"Of course she does."

Lanchee cleaned the cut and closed it with seven stitches. She studied the pattern before she touched the scanner.

"Three parallel lacerations," she said. "Deep at the wrist, shallow toward the elbow. No torn tendon. The nerve beside it is bruised, not cut."

The scanner completed its pass.

Every detail matched.

Lanchee looked at the result for half a second too long.

"Lucky," she said.

"I have been called worse."

She tied the final stitch. "You are jumpy. More than blood loss explains."

"Bad dream. Bad climb."

"You would tell me if something strange was happening aboard this ship."

It was not quite a question.

Jace thought about two invisible animals, a duck feather, the thing in the plumbing ice, and three camera systems that agreed none of them existed.

"Nothing stranger than usual."

Lanchee held his gaze. Then she released his arm.

Jace added the medical scan to his private file. He encrypted the feather's sample tag and disabled cloud indexing. He checked the spire hatch that night, then again on the last night before arrival.

The shaft stayed empty.

Cerberus still would not cross its threshold.
