# Chapter 24. The Leech

> *Most people do not know this, but the difference between a parasite and a patient is obvious until both of them are dying and only one can tell you to stop.*
>
> Jace Grant, *unpublished notes*

The parasite uncoiled one tendril from the miner's spine.

Its spiral mouth turned toward Jace.

Sophia tightened her hand on his arm.

"We promised," she said.

The man beneath it gave off another weak pulse of light. The parasite flattened across him and drank.

Jace wanted to tear it loose.

He wanted the clean moral simplicity of an attack, the kind with a target and a direction. The creature did not give him one. It lay on a helpless man. Every tendril crossed through the body they were trying to protect.

Jace stepped back.

The creature's mouth followed.

"{Aura}, return marker. Now."

His implant translated the intent into a physical alert. Sophia pulled them across.

##

Jace hit the examination-room floor hard enough to bruise his shoulder.

Lanchee was already kneeling beside him.

"What did you see?"

He looked at Sophia.

She was pale even for Sophia. She pushed herself upright and opened the notebook to a clean page.

"A living structure," she said. "Approximately one meter long and flattened across the patient's wound. Amber-gold. Multiple tendrils appear to enter the neck and spine. His visible energy decreases when the structure pulses."

Lanchee did not ask what visible energy meant.

"Is it physical?"

"Not in the way we are," Jace said.

"Can you remove it?"

"Maybe."

"What happens to the tendrils?"

"We do not know."

"What happens to him?"

"We do not know."

Lanchee stood.

"Then you do not touch it."

Jace followed her into the medical bay. Through the isolation window, the miner looked smaller than he had in the cafeteria. Tubes ran beneath the blanket. The wound from shoulder to ribs had closed into a dark seam, but the muscles around it had wasted.

His monitor showed an ordinary sleeping heart. A slightly low temperature. Slow respiration. No alarm.

"The system thinks he is improving," Jace said.

"The system measures repair," Lanchee said. "The repair is real. So is the decline."

The man's hand moved against the sheet.

His heart rate dropped by four beats.

The display remained green.

"How long?" Sophia asked.

"At the current trend, the model says indefinite stability. I disagree. Hours, perhaps a day before organ involvement."

"Can he consent?"

"He has not been conscious for six hours. His medical directive authorizes emergency experimental treatment when standard care fails. I am the attending physician."

Jace looked at the man's closed eyes.

"If we touch it, the thing may attack."

"You said it already looked at you."

"Yes."

"Then it can perceive the method you are using."

"Yes."

"Can it perceive this room?"

Jace thought of the red-eyed creature folding around a cable and turning toward Cerberus.

"Probably."

Lanchee checked the patient's trend again.

"I want one minute of observation with a synchronized medical clock. If the patient declines during a pulse, you may attempt separation. If he does not, you return. You do not improvise beyond separating the structure from the patient."

"It may not give us time," Jace said.

"Then you decide whether that is evidence or impatience."

Sophia wrote the authorization in her notebook. Lanchee signed it.

Jace signed beneath her.

"Cerberus stays here," he said.

The dog stood at the hatch, trembling with the effort of remaining still.

"He will not like that," Sophia said.

"I do not like taking him near something with a mouth."

Lanchee clipped a lead to Cerberus's harness.

"I have him."

Jace crouched and pressed his forehead to the dog's.

"Guard the doctor."

Cerberus licked his nose.

It was not agreement, but it was close enough.

Sophia caught Jace before he returned to the equipment.

"We need abort conditions," she said.

"Lanchee gave us one minute."

"That is a time limit, not an abort condition. If the tendrils tighten, if the patient's light falls sharply, if the creature divides, or if either of us is attacked, we return."

"If it attacks while attached to him, returning leaves it there."

"If we react without understanding, we may pull part of him out with it."

Jace looked through the glass. The miner's chest rose beneath the blanket.

"If his physical monitor falls during a pulse, I separate it."

"After we identify the release phase."

"If there is time."

Sophia held his gaze.

"You are angry at the creature."

"It is eating someone."

"Yes. Anger may make you act fast. It does not make the fast action correct."

"And caution may let it finish."

"Yes."

Neither of them apologized.

Sophia wrote both risks beneath Lanchee's authorization. They signed again, this time beside their own disagreement.

##

They changed the instrumentation before crossing again.

Jace fixed the physical medical clock to an isolated light that flashed once per second. Sparky carried its Astral camera and a paper wheel marked with sixty black lines. Sophia would rotate the wheel one mark for each physical flash that {Aura} reported through Jace's implant.

The method was crude. Every component failed differently. That made the combined failure useful.

Nick arrived while Jace was checking the wire leads.

He looked from the isolation window to Lanchee's signed authorization.

"You found the danger," he said.

"One of them."

"And you still cannot tell me what it is."

"Not yet."

Nick's jaw tightened.

"Then I am only checking the equipment."

"Understood."

He verified each clock, isolated {Aura}'s buffer, and locked the clinic connection to receive-only.

"If your implant temperature rises, the system pulls you out of the feed," he said. "If your physical heart becomes irregular, Lanchee ends the test. I do not care what you think you are about to accomplish."

"Understood."

Nick looked at him for another second.

"Come back and continue being a bad friend."

"That is the plan."

Sophia touched the bread.

The cold opened.

##

The parasite had moved.

Its body still covered the miner's wound, but its head now faced the wall behind which Jace and Sophia appeared. It had been waiting.

The paper wheel turned.

One second.

Sparky's camera showed a broken amber shape against a field of blue noise.

Two seconds.

The patient's light pulsed. A green thread moved toward the old wound.

The parasite's tendrils tightened.

The green vanished into its body.

Three seconds.

The physical medical signal reached {Aura}. Heart rate down. Temperature down by one hundredth of a degree.

Four.

Another pulse. Another decrease.

Sophia watched the tendrils.

"It may be attached to the injury," she said. "Pulling could reopen structures we cannot see."

"It is draining him."

"Yes. We need the direction of attachment."

Five.

Jace moved around the isolation room. The walls had almost no depth here. He could see the parasite's underside folded into the man's damaged shoulder. Fine tendrils entered along the healed seam. Three larger ones descended toward the spine.

Six.

The spiral mouth opened.

The thing did not have eyes. Jace knew it was watching him anyway.

Seven.

"{Aura}, trend."

The response arrived as a pattern in his visual field.

**DECLINE CORRELATES WITH PULSE. FOUR OF FOUR.**

Eight.

"Authorized," Jace said.

"Wait," Sophia said. "The upper tendrils release between pulses. If we separate then, there may be less damage."

Nine.

The parasite contracted.

Ten.

It released.

"Now," Sophia said.

Jace caught the flattened edge with both hands.

The surface compressed like cartilage wrapped around wire. It was cold enough to numb him through contact. He pulled upward.

The small tendrils slid free.

The three at the spine held.

The parasite folded in half.

Its mouth opened beside Jace's face.

He threw up his left arm.

The jaws closed around his forearm.

Pain arrived in both worlds.

In the Astral, the mouth tightened through his glow. In the physical clinic, his sleeve split beneath the cuff and blood struck the floor.

Jace did not let go.

"Sophia!"

She stood with her father's pen aimed along the parasite's body.

For one impossible instant, nothing moved.

Then the space around the pen compressed.

The parasite slammed sideways against the pale plane of the examination table. Its jaws opened. Jace tore his arm free and pulled again.

One spinal tendril released.

The parasite twisted toward Sophia.

She moved the pen a second time.

The force was narrower now. It struck beneath the flattened body and peeled it away from the miner. The remaining tendrils came free in a spray of amber light.

The man brightened.

The parasite rose on six jointed limbs.

It was not shaped like the black creature in Fortuna's wall. It was smaller, denser, and built around the spiral mouth. Burnt orange spread through its body as it gathered itself to jump.

Sophia's hand shook.

Jace saw the mistake before she made it. She was aiming at the creature.

"The table," he said.

She understood.

Her pen drove downward.

The examination table persisted here as a thin, ordered plane. It snapped against the floor, trapping the parasite between two structures it could not easily pass through.

The creature thrashed. Its limbs split into possible positions. The spiral mouth opened and closed around nothing.

Jace grabbed the copper camera lead and wrapped it over the table.

"Pull."

Sophia pulled with him.

The copper line brightened. The parasite compressed beneath it. Burnt orange flashed white.

Then the creature came apart.

It did not bleed. Its body collapsed inward as if all its surfaces had been released at once. Amber fragments lifted from the floor, circled each other, and joined into a sphere no larger than a plum.

The orb floated above the paper wheel.

Sophia stared at it. She raised one hand, and the sphere moved with her fingers.

"Do not touch it," Jace said.

"It is already responding to me."

"That is not a reason."

"If we leave it beside the patient, we do not know what it will become."

Sophia closed her hand around the sphere.

Amber light flowed through her fingers.

Sophia gasped.

The sphere vanished.

For a moment she shone brighter than anyone in the clinic. Warm gold moved through her arms and gathered behind her ribs. Her expression held terror, wonder, and the concentration of a scientist trying to take notes while falling.

"Sophia?"

"Warm," she said. "It is very warm."

The paper wheel turned to twenty-three.

Behind them, the miner's dim light expanded with his next breath.

{Aura} marked the physical monitor.

**HEART RATE RISING. TEMPERATURE RISING.**

Jace's forearm burned.

Blood spread across the floor around his physical body.

"Return," he said.

##

Lanchee caught Jace's injured arm before he finished becoming heavy.

Pain dropped him to one knee. Four curved wounds crossed his forearm. They were too wide for teeth and too evenly spaced for machinery.

Nick stared at the blood.

"There was nothing near you."

Jace looked through the isolation window.

The miner's monitor remained green. Heart rate up six beats. Temperature up two tenths. The man drew a deeper breath.

"We separated it," Jace said.

Lanchee injected anesthetic around the wounds.

"What bit you?"

"The structure."

"The structure you said was not physical."

"I was wrong."

"That is not a mechanism."

"I know."

Sophia sat against the opposite wall. She held both hands over her chest. Color had returned to her face, and sweat stood along her hairline.

"Check her first," Jace said.

"I am checking the person losing blood first."

"I absorbed something," Sophia said.

Lanchee looked at her.

"Define absorbed."

"I cannot yet."

"Then both of you are staying here."

Nick remained beside the empty patch of floor where Jace's arm had opened. His glasses replayed the physical camera feed.

"Twenty-three seconds," he said. "No object. No impact. Your tissue deformed inward before the skin broke."

He looked at Jace.

"You knew this could happen."

"I knew something might attack. I did not know it could cross the effect into my body."

"You left out another impossible part."

"Yes."

Nick turned away.

Lanchee closed the deepest wound and wrapped Jace's forearm. She scanned Sophia twice and found nothing outside ordinary stress. That did not comfort any of them.

The bite pattern continued beneath Jace's skin.

Ultrasound showed four narrow channels compressed through muscle without a conventional puncture path. The tissue at each channel looked pinched rather than cut. Lanchee changed probes, recalibrated, and obtained the same image.

"No foreign material," she said. "No tooth fragments. No residue."

"Good," Jace said.

"Unknown does not mean good."

She took blood for culture, inflammatory markers, radiation screening, and everything else the clinic could process locally. Then she injected broad-spectrum prophylaxis around a wound caused by an organism that might not possess cells.

Sophia stood beside the bed, still holding warmth behind her ribs.

"I can feel it when I look at the injury," she said.

Lanchee's attention sharpened. "Feel what?"

"A direction. Perhaps a connection. I do not know."

Jace watched gold gather faintly beneath the skin of her hand. He could not have said whether he truly saw it or remembered the Astral.

"No," he said.

Sophia looked at him.

"We wrote the rule," he said. "No changing a person without understanding and consent. I am conscious enough to consent, but neither of us understands."

The gold faded.

Sophia let out a breath. "Correct."

It cost her something to agree. Not pride. Hope.

Lanchee recorded the exchange without comment. She added a second line to Jace's treatment order: **No unclassified intervention, including by patient request.**

For two hours, the miner's temperature climbed toward normal.

His implant spikes stopped.

The medical system revised its model from stable recovery to accelerated recovery, then asked Lanchee to classify the unexplained change. She entered **unknown external intervention** and refused every suggested alternative.

The miner woke near the end of the second hour.

He asked for water.

Lanchee raised the bed and held the cup while he drank. His eyes moved across the room, stopping on Jace's bandaged arm and Sophia's open notebook.

"Accident?" he asked.

"During your treatment," Lanchee said.

The miner looked at Jace again. "You medical?"

"Delivery contractor."

"Fortuna has become strange while I slept."

His voice was weak, but the corner of his mouth moved.

"Do you remember the original injury?" Lanchee asked.

"Survey line jammed. I reached behind the guide. Something hit my shoulder."

"What?"

"Nothing on camera. Nothing in the suit log. I thought cable until they showed me the suit."

"Any sensation since then?"

He closed his eyes.

"Heavy. Every time I slept, something sat on my back. I would wake more tired."

Sophia's pen moved.

"Did it have a shape?" she asked.

"A bad dream has whatever shape you give it."

Lanchee took the cup when his hand began to shake.

"Enough questions."

The miner settled against the pillow. Before sleep took him again, he looked at Jace's arm.

"Whatever your delivery was," he said, "thank you."

Jace did not know how to explain that the delivery had been subtraction.

The monitor continued its patient green rhythm behind him without wavering.

He did not know Jace. He did not know Sophia. He had no memory of the wound opening or of anything attached to his back.

Lanchee gave him water and told him only that his condition had improved.

When she returned to the examination room, she closed the door.

"Your method found a cause that every medical system missed," she said. "It also produced a real wound without a physical instrument. The patient improved after your intervention. Those facts do not cancel one another."

"No," Sophia said.

"You will give me every record. You will not repeat this on another patient without me. You will not describe it as safe."

"Agreed," Jace said.

"And you will tell me what you are calling the structure."

Jace looked at the spiral he had drawn from memory.

"A leech."

Sophia shook her head.

"Too imprecise. Its mouth was dominated by repeated compressive geometry. A tick, perhaps."

"A Tick-Maw."

She considered it.

"Still imprecise. Memorable, though."

Jace wrote the name beneath the drawing.

The first line under it was not a description of the creature.

It was the cost.

**One patient recovering. One physical injury. One unknown absorption.**

Below that, he wrote the next requirement.

**Find out how many more are aboard Fortuna.**
