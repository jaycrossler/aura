# X-09d — Moving House

**Placement:** Follows X-09c, runs concurrently across several sessions
rather than a single scene — this is a montage-shaped beat.

## The strategy

Once the composition rule (X-09b) is in hand, Jace stops trying to move
complex finished devices across and starts rebuilding simplified versions on
the other side instead — same function, cheaper materials, fewer alloyed
parts. A sensor rebuilt in aluminum and copper crosses for a fraction of the
cost of the original steel-and-electronics unit.

He's also quietly making the Foundry's print queue more automated — a project
he was already chipping away at for its own sake — and using the efficiency
gains to "hide" the extra print jobs inside normal workshop throughput. He's
not stealing materials or falsifying manifests; he's just no longer the
bottleneck he used to be, and nobody's auditing where the slack in his own
schedule goes. This should read as clever, not criminal — the same instinct
that had him printing daily 1-Token plates for Maureen's grift back on the
*Falcon*.

First item across, once the technique is solid: a simple thermometer sensor.

## Sophia's inverse trick

Once the thermometer is set up on the Astral side, Sophia demonstrates the
opposite of her pen-cooling trick:

> "Look — if I do the opposite, and concentrate on the iron, I'll try to make
> it vibrate. Chaotic. Fast." She watches the thermometer, not the metal.
> "Look, it's getting warmer!" A pause, her shoulders dropping. "Oh, that's
> exhausting."

Jace tries and gets nothing. His frustration from X-09c recurs here, lighter
this time — he's used to it now, or getting used to it, but it still bothers
him that his mind "just doesn't work that way." Keep this brief; it's a
running texture beat across this stretch of chapters, not a single
confrontation.

**Must accomplish:**
1. Jace's rebuild-don't-transfer strategy is shown as an engineering decision
   with a clear cost logic (cheaper materials = cheaper crossing, per X-09b's
   rule), not just convenience
2. The workshop-automation cover is shown as clever resourcefulness, in
   keeping with Jace's established grift instincts — not deception played for
   guilt or tension
3. The thermometer crossing is small and successful — a working proof, not a
   struggle
4. Sophia's heating trick is shown as costly (visible exhaustion), mirroring
   the established cost of her cooling trick
5. Jace's inability to replicate her tricks recurs as a light, consistent
   character beat — not escalated into a new conflict here

**Must NOT do:**
- Make the automation-hiding read as illicit or risky — it should feel
  clever and slightly funny, consistent with Jace's characterization
- Resolve why Jace can't do what Sophia does
- Use `[Skill]` bracket notation (pre-convention)
