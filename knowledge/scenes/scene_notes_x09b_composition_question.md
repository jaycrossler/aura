# X-09b — The Composition Question

**Placement:** After X-09 (First Successful Transport). X-09 establishes that
Jace can move devices roughly 10x better than Sophia and that neither of them
knows why. This scene is Sophia solving it — the empirical derivation of the
atomic-weight-squared crossing-cost rule already canonical in
`spec_dimensional_travel_mechanics.md`, discovered on-page rather than stated
as exposition (per the open Arc 1 goal "Empirical Discovery of Rules").

**Reveal control:** Ch15 may establish only that equal masses of different materials
feel different and that assembled complexity also matters. It must not quantify the
relationship or identify the atomic-weight-squared rule. The controlled
helium/lithium/carbon experiment in X-09b remains the on-page quantitative discovery.

## The physics asides (recurring texture, not one scene)

Sophia thinks out loud while she works. These are scattered across several
exploration sessions leading up to and including this scene — pick natural
beats to drop them in rather than delivering them as a lecture:

> "The station uses centrifugal force — it spins and pushes us outward.
> Planets have centripetal force, where all the atoms in the planet pull us
> toward them. Same gravity feeling. Totally different processes."

> "String theory says we have ten dimensions. But every lab experiment we've
> ever run doesn't let us enter one — almost like we're being blocked. There
> are papers on it. Somehow, I think we've crossed into one, but our whole
> body did it. It should be happening all the time, particle by particle. Not
> all at once, like this."

> "For a hundred years we've known the quantum theories don't line up.
> There's always been something missing." *(gestures at the visible Will
> threading through people's thoughts)* "It's like this — fluid, or energy,
> or whatever links it all together. But why only see it now? All the way out
> here?"

These are the seeds of her later papers. She's not performing insight for
Jace's benefit — she's thinking, and he happens to be there. He mostly
doesn't have a response beyond "huh" or a follow-up engineering question,
which is correct; he's not supposed to keep pace with her here.

## The experiment

Once the 10x differential from X-09 is sitting unresolved between them,
Sophia's instinct is to control for variables the way Jace already has
(mass, shape, complexity) and isolate the one nobody's tested: **what the
object is made of.**

She sets up three identical containers, each holding exactly one gram:
liquid helium, lithium, and carbon. Same mass, same container, same
conditions — composition is the only variable.

She has Jace pull each one across, timing the effort and the fatigue cost.
Helium comes almost free. Lithium costs noticeably more. Carbon costs more
again — not linearly with mass (they're all one gram), which is the tell.

Sophia works it from atomic numbers, thinking aloud with the kind of facts
she just has on hand:

> "An atom is about a tenth of a nanometer. A virus is about fifty nanometers.
> A human hair is about a tenth of a millimeter." She's already doing the
> math before she finishes the sentence. "If the cost scales with... give me
> a minute."

She lands on weight-squared, or close enough to it that Jace can validate it
empirically: she calculates a predicted cost/time for moving a full suit,
then a helmet (different alloy mix, different total mass), and has Jace
actually attempt both. His measured effort lines up with her prediction
closely enough that neither of them can call it coincidence.

**Must accomplish:**
1. The physics asides read as Sophia thinking, not teaching — Jace does not
   need to understand them, and the reader shouldn't be quizzed on them either
2. The experiment is shown as real methodology — controlled variable, repeated
   trials, prediction before test, validation after
3. This scene is the empirical origin point of the atomic-weight-squared rule
   already canonical in `spec_dimensional_travel_mechanics.md` — do not let
   Sophia simply state the rule; she should arrive at it messily, checking her
   own math against Jace's results
4. Leave the 10x differential itself (why Jace specifically pulls harder than
   Sophia) *unresolved* by this scene — composition explains cost-by-material,
   not the practitioner-type gap. That gap is a different, still-open question.

**Must NOT do:**
- Turn Sophia's asides into an infodump delivered directly to Jace or the reader
- Resolve why Jace outperforms Sophia specifically — this scene explains
  material cost, not practitioner asymmetry
- Use `[Skill]` bracket notation (pre-convention)
