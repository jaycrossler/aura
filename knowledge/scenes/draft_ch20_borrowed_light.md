# Chapter 20. Borrowed Light

> *Athena: Artemis still has not answered.*
>
> *Apollo: I know.*
>
> *Athena: You are like right next to her. Can't you just radio her?*
>
> *Apollo: Didn't you take astronomy? We're 30 million miles away this week. I can't just radio her. I'm trying every message route though.*
>
> *Athena: Try sleeping too. I saw you on the Pig cam yesterday. You look awful.*
>
> *Apollo: I'm fine.*
>
> *Athena: Liar.*

The next morning, Sophia met him at 0430.

She had already crossed when he reached her room. Her physical cabin was empty. A cold page waited beneath the janky relay.

**PUSH OVER PROBE.**

Jace looked at the empty space where she should have been. He put a coffee on her table for when she returned, one of the last he could afford. A peace offering.

*Ready,* he texted to the telegraph, hoping she was there to catch it.

He pushed over a three-meter plastic rod, a wired thermometer, a humidity sensor, and the best gas detector he could design.

Sophia waited beside Sparky in the Astral room. She had mounted the camera controller herself. The three lenses faced three incompatible directions.

"Good morning," Jace said.

"The controller works. Camera two does not."

"Good morning to two-thirds of the cameras."

Sophia pointed at the pole. She was in a mood.

He attached the thermometer to the end and ran its wire back to a physical needle display. The device had no network, no clock, and no opportunity to improve its answer.

As planned, he pushed the pole through the cabin wall.

The wall did not resist. From the Astral, its physical material existed as a narrow exposed section. Jace moved the probe around it in the extra direction and extended the sensor into open space beyond the *Victoria's* physical hull.

The needle fell.

Minus nineteen point eight Celsius.

Jace checked the scale.

"Again," Sophia said.

He withdrew the probe. The needle rose to minus seven.

He extended it through another wall.

Minus twenty point one.

"New construction begins cold and nearly empty."

"And our lab is becoming a carbon-dioxide trap."

"We add circulation and chemical scrubbers."

"In both places. We shouldn't stay here if we don't have to. Maybe that's adding to headaches."

She wrote the readings without looking at him, added tasks to their TO-DO queue.

Jace waited until she finished, then steeled himself and finally asked her.

"Are you angry because I kept working?"

Sophia's pen stopped.

"You took something from me."

"By accident," Jace said.

"I know."

He looked stricken. "I am so sorry."

"I felt as if two days of my life had been removed in one second. Then you asked whether we could move another machine."

"You said you were ok."

"I was not clear. You were full of what you took, and I was on the floor. Neither of us should have trusted my yes."

Jace looked at the distance she maintained between them.

"No more tests after an unexpected transfer," he said. "Not until both people have recovered and agreed again."

"That would have helped yesterday."

"I know. I am sorry."

They both crossed back. As he left for work, he noticed she had at least drunk the coffee. Progress?

##

Suzi came to Drone Ops after lunch.

She waited until Carlos left for Factory Four, then placed an archive token beside Jace's console.

"I found footage that {Seek} indexed as routine medical movement," she said.

"Current?"

"Old. Two days after Helena disappeared."

Jace opened the file.

The camera belonged to a repair drone moving through Ring Three at 0214. The corridor was nearly empty. Construction lights cycled in alternate sections to save power.

Yáo and another miner emerged from an unlisted service passage.

Between them hung a flexible black bag almost two meters long.

The bag sagged at the hips and shoulders.

At one end, the fabric tightened around a heel.

Jace stopped the footage.

"Again," Suzi said.

He played it frame by frame. The drone's focus softened before the men crossed. Three frames lost detail. The bag returned closer, its center shifting with a weight that was not rigid equipment.

It looked like a body.

"The privacy classifier suppressed the image," Jace said. "It tagged the bag as a covered medical load."

"Beacon?"

"None. No destination tag either."

"Can you prove what is inside?"

"No."

"Then write about the camera. Not the shape."

Jace wrote the technical note: automated privacy suppression, absent medical beacon, absent destination, intact source frames retained.

He did not write *body*. He was stressed and angry.

Suzi removed the token and left in a hurry.

Nick's reply arrived after she left.

*Correct. It was a bad apology.*

Nothing followed.

##

The Long Burn was loud enough to turn privacy into exhaustion. Jace had stopped by his room to feed Cerberus, then saw Hayes and stopped for a beer. The old man waved and tossed Cerberus a small piece of something. Cerberus yipped and jumped a meter into the air to catch it. Everyone nearby applauded. The dog took his prize to the corner, plainly proud of himself. Hayes bought him another beer, which hit his sleep-deprived mind a little harder then he expected.

The market had stopped serving full dinners due to some supply issues, so the bar sold stew beside the beer. Every table was occupied. One screen showed the American Red administration blaming Chinese sabotage for the missing ships. Another showed a Chinese official blaming an American blockade for the supply failures. The captions disagreed about which government had moved armed ships first. 

After Suzi had left, Jace looked for other drone footage and found multiple times Jin, Yáo, and the others had been moving into the depressurized construction zone, carrying boxes and bags in pressure suits across the airless gap. They always avoided station cameras, but the delivery drones saw alot.

Jace took a stool at the counter alone. Hayes had already gotten up, complaining about an old man needing to head to bed, and the seat beside Jace stayed empty. He ordered a third beer he couldn't really afford and didn't care. He was in a foul mood.

The market had stopped serving full dinners due to supply issues, so the bar sold stew beside the beer. Every other table was occupied. One screen showed the American Red administration blaming Chinese sabotage for the missing ships. Another showed a Chinese official blaming an American blockade for the supply failures. The captions disagreed about which government had moved armed ships first.

The beer hadn't improved Jace's mood. He had spent the walk over rehearsing what he'd tell Suzi later, and hating how thin it sounded even in his own head. Sophia seeing one of the things had been too much. If someone was keeping one on the station, it was dangerous, and he had no proof of anything, only a pattern he couldn't stop assembling.

And of course the fates aligned. Jin and Yáo stood near the counter with two other miners. Yáo was jittery. Broad shoulders, scarred hands, quiet posture. He wore no belt axe inside the bar. The absence made him look prepared rather than harmless.

Jace should have left it alone. He had not slept. Sophia would barely talk to him. Nick wouldn't acknowledge him. Public screens had spent two days supplying a nationality for every failure, and here was someone involved, standing at the bar, close enough to say something to.

"Asteroid minerals are going missing," Jace said, louder than he meant to. "You're moving unmanifested loads. Systems fail after you are there."

Yáo turned.

"What did you say?" His accent was thick, his voice flat.

Jace didn't let himself stop. "At what point does a pattern become sabotage? My sister is on Adama Station."

"American drone man watches Chinese workers and messes up their oxygen supply, then wants to know about patterns."

"My automation delayed everyone. I apologized for that."

"Not to me."

"Do you deserve an appology?"

Yáo hit him.

The first punch split Jace's lip and surprised him so much he froze.

He blocked the next strike with both forearms, remembering football training as a kid. He stepped inside Yáo's reach, and drove a shoulder forward.

Mars-standard gravity made the movement look better than it was.

They both went flying. Yáo turned gracefully in the air, landed on one foot, and used Jace's sleeve to continue the rotation. Jace struck the floor on his back.

The room flashed white.

He rolled. Yáo's boot missed his ribs and caught his hip.

Jace got one hand around Yáo's wrist. Yáo broke the grip without hurrying and hit him twice in the body. The second strike emptied Jace's lungs.

Jace threw one punch. It caught Yáo high on the cheek, little more than a slap.

Yáo hooked Jace's ankle, turned his shoulder, and put him down again. Jace tried to rise. A knee settled between his shoulder blades.

He had lost. The room went silent.

Cerberus came charging out from the corner, snarling.

"Stay," Jace gasped.

The dog stopped, every line of him aimed at Yáo, growling.

A hand closed on Yáo's collar and pulled him back so fast he stumbled.

Jin Lóng did not raise his voice. He didn't need to; the bar had already gone quiet around them, the way a room goes quiet when the person who can actually end something walks into it.

"Enough," he said. Just the one word, and Yáo stood down like it weighed more than any shout would have.

Jin looked at Jace on the floor, split lip, already swelling. He didn't offer a hand up. He didn't apologize either.

"You accuse my crew in a bar," Jin said, "where every screen is already telling people who to hate this week. That is not evidence. That is convenient."

"I didn't accuse anyone of anything I can't back up."

"You said a pattern. A pattern is not an accusation. It is also not nothing." Jin's eyes stayed level, unreadable. "Go home, Mr. Grant. Put ice on that."

He turned Yáo toward the door with a hand on his shoulder, said something low and fast in Mandarin that made Yáo's jaw tighten, and walked him out without looking back.

{Penny} issued Jace a public-disturbance notice before he'd even gotten his feet under him.

As he left towards Two, he had 8 tokens left in his account.

##

Sophia opened the *Victoria's* hatch, saw his face, and laughed.

It was the first warm sound she had made around him in days.

"I lost a fistfight."

"The evidence currently supports that conclusion. Weren't you in the military?"

Jace touched his split lip and regretted it.

"I was Air Force. I pushed keys to fly drones on the other side of the world."

"No one punched you through the screen?"

"The military would consider that a security vulnerability."

Her smile faded before he finished.

She was still angry, but he could see it warring with pity.

"Should we still go?"

He nodded. "It's not as bad as it looks. I left Cerberus in the barn, he was too upset."

Earlier, they had agreed to see the thing on Ring Three, or at least get an image that was not only her own eyes doubting themselves. Something he could take to Suzi.

"We are not going into the construction area," she said. "We are just looking."

Jace clipped the third camera housing back onto Sparky's frame with tape because the mount had stripped its threads. "Recordings. Not contact."

He picked up a large steel crowbar that he'd previously moved over and hooked it to his belt. Just in case.

They left for Ring Two. From the vestibule, he could barely see up into the bar on One, where people still congregated. Thirty meters below and a hundred, perhaps a hundred fifty around the curve, the walkable area of Ring Three ended in construction gantries. From here, it should have been nothing but distant motion and worker traffic. The blocked bands of copper and silver showed black through the fractal landscape, while everything else formed blue-tinted fractals.

Between pipes and wires, they could see the level on Three taking form. Jace rebooted and resynched Sparky's third camera, which finally agreed with the other two.

They took the stairs down to Three and Jace angled the feed. Scaffolding. A crane. Two suited figures moving cargo. Ordinary.

Then he saw a supply run near the back of the airless construction space, close to the passage that had drawn Suzi's suspicion. It was not on the map. A small unmarked drone, unlike any Fortuna hull pattern he recognized, carried a load of flat panels toward the room. Insulating foil covered each one but did nothing to hide the material beneath it in the Astral.

The panels didn't glow the way most cargo did. They didn't glow at all. Wherever one passed, the faint Astral texture of the wall behind it smeared and dimmed, the way a bright window looks when someone draws a heavy curtain across it mid-glance.

"That's not steel," Jace said. "That's silver."

"Are they panelling the entire room in silver? I can't see anything inside it."

It wasn't only cargo. Twice while they watched, a suited figure crossed from the construction gantry to the unlisted room's hatch on a tether line, moving with the unhurried economy of someone who'd made the same short trip a hundred times before. Once, a second figure met the first partway, passed across something Jace couldn't identify at this range, small enough to fit in one glove, and went back the way they'd come. Routine. Practiced. The kind of traffic pattern that only develops when people are somewhere often enough to stop thinking about how it looks from outside.

"That's not a delivery route," Sophia said. "That's a commute."

Movement pulled his attention off the room; something in the construction zone shifted in a way that wasn't a worker's rhythm, too much of it in too little space, folding into itself and back out.

"There," Sophia said, quiet.

Jace zoomed in. The image blurred and refused to resolve into anything he could name. A shape. A wrongness in how the shape used the space it occupied.

"Drone," he said, out of habit more than conviction. "Has to be. I'm zooming Sparky in on it."

The wall tore open eight meters to their left.

Jace had time to think *that is not where the wall is supposed to open* before something came out of it.

It moved faster than anything mechanical had any right to, closing the distance before either of them reacted. He finally saw it in the bright astral light. Not a robot. Rounded textured tissue where he expected housing. Sharp arms, too many of them. A limb that bent in three places no metal limb could emulate. An eye that was not a lens, because a lens does not have multiple lids or ooze pus.

It caught him across the forearm and side. Pain arrived half a second behind the sight of his own blood in the Astral light. For the second time in two hours, he was knocked to the ground.

Sophia was already moving, already shouting something he couldn't process over the ringing that had started behind his eyes.

It was not a drone.

It had never been a drone.
