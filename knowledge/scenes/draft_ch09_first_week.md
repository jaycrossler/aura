# Chapter 9. Eyes Open

> *Most people do not know this, but a small station can hold two versions of the truth. One goes in the public report. The other stops talking when you enter the room.*
>
> Jace Grant, *Fortuna notes*

The evening run began at 1800 and did not officially exist.

No signup. No route permit. No organizer. At 1758, people drifted into Ring Two wearing soft shoes and old shirts. At 1800, someone started moving. Everyone else followed.

The loop covered Ring Two, the Commons, and the Foundry level. One point four kilometers. Jace joined near the end of his first week and was the fastest runner by a clear margin. Earth muscles helped alot even with the weighted vest.

People watched from shopfronts. Children counted laps. A baker handed out water at the Commons. Two women from a fabrication crew matched Jace's pace for half a circuit and asked whether he needed someone to show him the Sky Gardens after hours.

"Carlos already showed me," Jace said.

They looked at each other.

"Of course he did," one said.

They dropped back laughing. Jace assumed he had missed a station joke.

The crowd changed from night to night. The first evening felt like a festival. The second felt like an argument waiting for a reason, as tensions were rising.

Public screens carried footage from Odysseus Station. A cargo lock had failed during a Chinese-chartered transfer. Two workers were dead. The cause remained under investigation.

The headline did not.

SUSPECTED CHINESE SABOTAGE DISRUPTS BELT SUPPLY LINE.

The Chinese-language summary used different wording. {Seek} helpfully translated it as "Heroes of China gave life for the people", but his HUD translator said something much darker. A group of Chinese barn-workers near the screen left together. An American shift crew watched them go.

The runners passed Helena's memorial on every lap.

Someone added fresh flowers. Someone else added a printed card that read COME HOME. The station did not call it a shrine. Everyone slowed near it.

Jace dreamed harder on run nights.

Crystal corridors opened inside the station. He saw rooms he had never entered, stacked through each other like transparent boxes. Once he dreamed of a wide observation gallery with a cracked mural of Earth along one wall.

Two days later, Carlos took him through Ring One and pointed out the same gallery.

The crack crossed the Atlantic in exactly the same place as in his dream.

Jace stopped walking.

"You all right?" Carlos asked.

"First time we came here?"

"For you, yes."

Jace looked at the mural.

"Thought I saw it on a station map."

"You probably did."

Carlos accepted the answer. Jace did not.

##

Suzi Gonzales found him at the Long Burn Bar after his third run.

She was compact, gray-haired, and built like someone who still expected fitness tests after retirement. She asked if the seat across from him was free, then sat before he answered.

"Suzi. Security head."

"Jace. Delivery. Apparently."

"I know."

Everyone knew.

Suzi bought the next round without asking. Jace drank slowly. He could not afford habits, and Suzi did not appear interested in creating one.

For an hour, she did not mention Helena.

She asked about the *Falcon*. Jace described Maureen's relationship with profit and plates. Suzi told him about her first year on Fortuna, when the station had one bar, two pressure leaks, and an informal law against letting founders repair anything after drinking.

Cerberus collected pretzel pieces from three tables.

Suzi noticed on the second piece and said nothing until the fifth.

"Your dog is running an unlicensed food network."

"I do not control him. I manage exceptions."

"Learning Mode."

"Chief."

She smiled. It changed her face more than Jace expected.

"Lieutenant." she replied. She knew about his military history, and he had guesses at hers from her bearing and haircut.

They stayed too long telling war stories. When Jace walked back to the boring inn, the station felt less like a job site and more like a place where someone knew he was there.

Suzi waited three days before she used that trust.

This time she joined him at the cafeteria's gray-soup table. No drink. No smile.

"You saw Helena's jacket."

"Carlos said security checked everything."

"We did. Now tell me what you meant when you asked about the walls."

Jace kept his face still. She had been watching. Base security. Of course.

He could tell her about the spire. The feather sat in a private evidence sleeve at the bottom of his sock drawer at the inn. He could show her footage of himself fighting empty air.

He imagined the medical hold, the Origin audit, and another bright message from {Alex} explaining that institutional concern carried no malice. {Alex} and the other station A.I.s would be watching them, even here.

"Ships hide voids," he said. "Old retrofits. Dead conduit. Spaces that exist on the blueprint and not the scan, or the other way around."

"Fortuna has thirty years of those. We opened every one near her pod."

Suzi folded her hands.

"Helena Reyes went home from Sophia Lotte's birthday party. Her pod sealed from inside, she used the bathroom. In the morning, Helena was gone. Tracking bracelet on the bed. Breathing gear missing. Doors weren't opened. No hatch cycle. No EVA record. No suit removed. No camera gap. No pressure event."

Jace ran through possibilities in his head.

"Maintenance access?"

"Logged and watched."

"Sensor spoof?"

"Three independent layers."

"Temperature from body heat? Air chemistry?"

"Normal until it was not occupied. No transition."

"Then its something impossible with known technology."

"That is the problem."

Suzi's voice stayed level. Her grief did not. Anger flared in her eyes.

"Are you following the sabotage story?"

"The damaged ship and Odysseus station?"

"Exactly. Did you know an unmanned chinese transport was the last ship on Odysseus before it suddenly had a decompression event and stopped responding to any comms?"

"No. Wait, you think the Chinese put something on people's ships? There were no explosions, right?"

"They make most of the robots. Robots can cause damage without explosions. They have military camoflague systems, so might not even be detectable. Who knows?"

Jace paused. That hit too close to home.

Suzi continued. "Helena knew everyone. Half the station wants Jin Lóng charged because they were always seen together and because his section keeps its records behind {Seek} and he was seen on camera near her room that night. The other half wants her declared dead so the inheritance process can start. I have no body, no breach, and no evidence that Jin did anything."

"Do you think he did?"

Suzi glanced toward the Chinese tables before answering.

"I think his people hold access I cannot audit. Their A.I. won't share anything. I also think suspicion is not evidence, even when it feels like it. I don't know.  But something fishy is going on."

She heard the edge in her own words. Her mouth tightened.

"That is my bias talking. I know what it sounds like. Knowing does not always shut it up."

Jace looked across the cafeteria.

Jin sat with Hayes near the Barn entrance. The Chinese miner was compact and heavily built, with a scar crossing one corner of his mouth. A belt axe hung at his hip. Hayes said something. Jin laughed politely.

The sound carried. Warm. Brief. Ordinary.

Mei sat two tables away from them, alone. She ate without appearing to watch.

"Why tell me?" Jace asked.

"You arrived after she vanished. Four months of transit logs put you farther from this case than any adult on Fortuna."

"A clean alibi. There were four of us on that ship."

"You were military."

"They kicked me out."

"You also run delivery drones through corridors my fixed cameras do not cover. I am not asking you to spy on Jin or his crew. I am asking you to notice things. A door open that should not be. A panel disturbed. A route that makes no sense. Bring it to me before you bring it to Origin."

"Why before Origin?"

"Because Origin closed the case as presumed accidental loss after twelve days. And China funds many Origin projects."

"Odysseus Station was AlphaCorp and European run, not Origin or Chinese."

"You see my problem."

Jace thought of the duck feather. He thought of a camera showing an empty conduit while his arm opened under invisible claws.

"Eyes open," he said.

"That is the whole job."

At the next table, a Philipino miner sat down and spoke over a bowl of noodles.

"Massive wound," the man said. "Shoulder to ribs. Suit intact. Somehow no pressure loss."

His companion told him to lower his voice, glancing around.

The conversation moved to overtime.

She stood, looking tired. She probably hadn't heard.

"Good night, Grant."

Jace sat with his untouched soup.

Across the room, Jin cocked his head as if listening to something. He stopped laughing. His head turned toward Jace, not quickly, not enough to count as a reaction.

Then he looked away.

##

That night, Jace dreamed of Helena.

She floated outside the station without a suit. A night shirt billowed around her in a wind that could not exist. The stars shone through her skin.

When Jace reached for her, she opened her eyes.

Something moved behind them.

Jace woke before he touched her.

Cerberus stood at the foot of the bunk, facing Jace. Staring at him with what looked like suspicion.

The dog gave one low growl.
