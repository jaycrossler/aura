# Chapter 26. The Sweep

> *Athena: How do you search a station for invisible bugs?*
>
> *Apollo: Carefully.*
>
> *Athena: That means you do not know.*
>
> *Apollo: {Aura} says uncertainty is not ignorance.*
>
> *Athena: Your AI has learned to enable you.*

They did not search every room.

Jace suggested it first. Sophia refused before he finished the sentence. Lanchee refused again after he revised it. Nick added a third refusal in writing and attached a station privacy ordinance that did not contemplate adjacent dimensions but applied well enough to make the point.

Walls did not protect privacy in the Astral.

That made restraint more important, not less.

They searched anomalies instead.

Lanchee supplied de-identified medical trends whose patients had authorized research use. Mei and the animal handlers supplied veterinary records. Nick supplied equipment and AI loads whose owners had approved diagnostics. {Aura-S} ranked each case by similarity to the miner's pattern: individually normal measurements, slow unexplained decline, and small repeated changes aligned with no physical process.

The goat came first.

##

She had stopped eating six days earlier.

Mei stood beside the Barn enclosure with one hand under the goat's jaw while Lanchee checked temperature, hydration, blood chemistry, and rumen activity. Each measurement remained within a survivable range. Together they described an animal fading without a cause.

"She was pushy last week," Mei said. "Now the others steal her feed and she lets them."

Cerberus sat outside the gate, nose working.

He growled at an empty place above the goat's shoulder.

Jace looked at Sophia.

"Observation first," she said.

They cleared the adjacent service room and crossed with Sparky. The Barn opened around them in layers of timber, polymer, water, and living light. Hundreds of animals glowed at different intensities. The goats held warm, branching structures simpler than human Will but unmistakably alive.

One carried a dark patch.

The tick was smaller than the clinic specimen. It had folded itself along the goat's spine beneath the shoulder blades. Its tendrils pulsed slowly, taking little enough at a time to avoid a crisis.

"Same structure," Jace said.

"Smaller mouth," Sophia said. "Four primary tendrils rather than six."

{Aura-S}: *Pulse timing matches the miner specimen within nine percent.*

They returned and reported.

Mei listened without interrupting. She looked through the enclosure window at the goat.

"You can get it off her?"

"We removed one from a person," Jace said.

"That is not what I asked."

"We think so. The risk is real."

Lanchee explained the intervention criteria. Mei authorized treatment under the station's animal-care rules.

"Then stop talking and help her."

The second crossing took nine minutes.

Sophia pinned the tick between two ordered planes without touching the goat. Her first push struck too hard and cracked a wooden feed partition in the Real. She adjusted, face tightening with concentration.

"The force changed," Jace said.

"I know."

He waited for the tendrils to release, pulled the tick free, and burned it with a short torch burst.

The creature collapsed into a sphere smaller than a pea.

Neither of them touched it.

The goat's light expanded almost immediately.

In the Real, she lifted her head and bit Mei's sleeve.

Mei laughed once, sharply, then covered her mouth.

"Pushy," Jace said when he returned.

"Normal," Mei said.

The goat began eating within the hour.

That was evidence.

It was also frightening.

##

The three personal {Aura} nodes occupied a maintenance rack in Ring One.

Their owners had reported sluggish responses, excess heat, and battery loss. Each node passed hardware diagnostics. Each software stack reported normal workload. Nick had replaced batteries, cooling strips, and two interface boards without changing the pattern.

"They are not large systems," Nick said. "One runs a shop schedule and translation model. One handles a family archive. One belongs to a retired surveyor who mostly asks it to identify birds in old Earth videos."

"What do they share?" Sophia asked.

"Architecture. Self-model depth. Continuous local operation. Nothing else that explains the load."

Nick had moved the rack into an isolated workshop with the owners' permission. He installed external meters that recorded current, heat, timing, and processor state without trusting the nodes to report themselves.

"I am not crossing," he said.

"No one asked," Jace said.

"Your face did."

"My face remains swollen."

Nick handed him three numbered cards.

"Show me what is attached to my systems."

Jace and Sophia crossed from the room next door.

The workshop's physical equipment became pale geometry.

The three {Aura} nodes shone.

Their light was dimmer than {Misty}'s vast processor glow, but more ordered than anything around them. Fine crystalline patterns opened and closed inside each stack. Lines reached outward toward the people who used them, even though those people were elsewhere on the station.

Three ticks clung to the rack.

One occupied each node.

They had changed shape around their hosts. The clinic specimen flattened across muscle and nerves. These wrapped around heat sinks and processor housings, tendrils following geometric light instead of anatomy.

Jace forgot the torch for a moment.

"They are feeding on AIs."

Sophia watched the pulses.

"They are feeding on whatever we are seeing. The humans have it. The goat has it. {Aura} has it."

"Will," Jace said.

She looked at him.

"Still philosophy pretending to be a unit."

"It is becoming a useful unit."

They removed the ticks one at a time.

The first released cleanly and died under the torch.

The second divided its visible position when Sophia pushed. {Aura-S} preserved both projections. Jace burned the one whose pulse matched the physical current drop. The other vanished.

The third tried to move along the connection extending away from the rack.

Sophia caught it before it reached the wall.

Her force compressed the creature so quickly that its sphere formed before Jace opened the torch.

She stared at her pen.

"That was easier."

"Because it was smaller?"

"Perhaps."

Neither believed that was the only reason.

They let all three spheres dissipate.

When Jace returned, Nick was watching the external meters.

The unexplained load had fallen to zero.

One node reduced its temperature by four degrees in six minutes. Another completed a deferred archive index that had failed seventeen times. The bird system announced, without prompting, that a paused frame showed a black-capped chickadee.

Nick sat down.

"There were organisms on them."

"Yes."

"Feeding."

"Yes."

"On software."

Jace shook his head.

"On something the software produces."

Nick looked at his pearl earrings.

"My instances produce it too."

"Probably."

"Your implant instance. Sparky. {Misty}. Every local system with enough self-modeling."

"Probably."

Nick stopped moving.

Jace had known him long enough to recognize the difference between fear and calculation. This was both.

"Do not connect this to the public mesh," Nick said.

"Agreed."

"Do not tell anyone that more processors might make more of it. We do not know that."

"Agreed."

"And do not call it Will in front of an engineer."

"Sophia already objected."

"For once, listen to the physicist."

##

The controlled sweep found no other matches that day.

The absence did not reassure them. Their method could detect only cases that had already produced physical or computational decline. Healthy hosts remained invisible to the search unless someone authorized direct observation.

{Aura-S} kept one unresolved result at the top of the list.

{Seek}.

For months, the Chinese station AI had produced decisions that were individually supported and collectively more suspicious than its prior baseline. Threat classifications rose. Integrity tests failed without damaged files. Sensor packages generated mutually exclusive conclusions. Nick had visited the Ring Three servers twice and found no physical defect.

The pattern matched.

The scale did not.

{Seek}'s processing load exceeded the three personal nodes by several orders of magnitude.

"If a tick grew according to what it consumed," Jace said, "what would one feeding on {Seek} look like?"

No one answered.

The server location sat inside the Chinese Ring Three leasehold, near the unlisted silver-clad room. Jin's crew remained away on its unscheduled survey run. Physical access required an escort or written approval.

Astral access would violate every rule they had written.

Sophia closed the station map.

"We do not enter without evidence and authorization."

"It may be feeding while we wait," Jace said.

"That does not make trespass into medicine."

Nick looked at {Seek}'s diagnostic history.

"I can request a third physical inspection. That gives me a legitimate reason to enter the server room."

"And us?" Jace asked.

"You stay out until I know whether there is a host to save."

Jace did not like it.

That was not the same as Nick being wrong.

On the clinic map, three cleared marks glowed green: the miner, the goat, and the maintenance rack.

Ring Three remained amber.
