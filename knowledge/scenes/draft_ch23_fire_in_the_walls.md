# Chapter 23. Fire in the Walls

> *Athena: I cannot believe you, the AI hater, put an AI in your brain.*
>
> *Apollo: {Aura} says, "Stubborn humans. Conquer. Destroy."*
>
> *Athena: Your jokes are worse than Dad's. Still nothing from Artemis?*

By the following evening, the torches and armor were waiting in the Astral *Victoria*.

They ate a quick dinner, Cerberus overjoyed at having all of his favorite humans in the same room.

The first person Jace and Sophia tried to pull across was Nikos.

He lasted forty-three minutes before opening one eye.

"I am not there," he said.

"You are not supposed to talk," Sophia said, "Relax."

"I have been not talking for an hour. I am now reporting a failed experiment."

"You are halfway across," she said.

"That is worse."

Nikos closed his eye again.

They sat in the *Victoria's* main room in a circle on the floor. Sophia held both of his wrists. They three wore light pressure suits stripped of everything they could safely leave behind. Nikos had surrendered his tablet, belt hardware, watch, earring computer, and six sensor patches after Sophia explained how every additional circuit slowed the crossing.

Brandon watched with a physical camera mounted on each wall and the expression of a man supervising surgery performed by people who had learned anatomy from social networking.

Nick sat beside him, narrating into the {Aura} on his wristcomp.

"His mass has dropped by eleven percent," Brandon said.

"Twenty," Nick said. "The floor scale averages too aggressively."

"I calibrated that scale."

"Then you calibrated it poorly, it should send updates faster."

Nikos opened his eye again.

"If I survive, I am separating you two."

Sophia squeezed his wrist.

"Quiet."

Jace was in the Astral, being the only one here he was talking with {Aura}, trying to come up with formulas for how they could better estimate the time to move mass. 

"It feels like Nikos is more... resistance rather than weight. Different than pushing things across."

{Aura}: *I'll add a hypothesis to the log that conscious beings behave differently than base matter.*

Pulling himself across had become familiar, like finding a handhold in darkness. Pulling another person felt he had to individually lift every cell in Nikos' body. Like lifting sand. He needed to somehow use his thoughts to get the sand to understand it needed to stick together.

Sophia found the steady pattern inside Nikos and pushed sideways against it. Jace strained and pulled, gently and insistently. Like he was pulling Nikos' entire form, not just body parts. For a few seconds, all three aligned.

The observation room lost depth.

Cold replaced warmth.

Nikos's hand became solid in Jace's grip.

The physical cameras saw him disappear, and Sparky's cameras in the astral saw his shape solidify. They were mounted as close to the same place and distance as the cameras in the Real so that they could build a model from the two different visual frames of reference.

In the Astral *Victoria*, Nikos opened both eyes and screamed.

He stopped himself quickly, but not quickly enough to preserve his dignity. Jace pretented to ignore it. Nikos adjusted his breathing mask around his beard, trying to stop the urge to hyperventalate.

"That," he said, standing up and breathing hard, "is much more uncomfortable than you said."

The observation room occupied more directions than it had in the Real. Its cabinets opened inward and sideways at the same time. The table showed every surface without turning. Brandon remained visible as overlapping color and structure, standing beside the place where Nikos's body no longer existed.

Nikos stared at him.

"He looks like a star collapsed into a nervous system."

Sophia 'appeared' on the couch, collapsing from her four-dimensional shape into a solid outline, like the revese of openning a very decorative package. 

"That is the single weirdest things I've ever seen," Nikos said.

"It gets weirder."

Cerberus stepped through, curled on the couch. He stood, shook himself, and sniffed Nikos's knee.

Nikos looked down at him.

"The dog is normal."

Cerberus jumped up and Nikos caught him. Cerberus licked his face rubbing the side of his muzzle on his beard and mask.

He coughed, "OK, normal for a dog."

They left Nikos aboard the Astral *Victoria* with Sophia, then Jace returned to the Real.

Nick had watched both transitions without making a joke. When Jace became fully physical again, Nick removed his pearl earrings and set them in a padded case. Then he took off his overlay glasses. He had already changed into sweatpants and a shirt.

He looked like a whole different person. Anxious.

"I still keep thinking this is an eleborate prank. I'm still mad at you, you know."

"You can stop," Jace said.

"I know. That is the only reason I haven't."

Nick closed his eyes.

The second crossing took longer. Jace was still sore and a bit tired from moving Nikos.

Nick fought it without moving. Jace felt him reject each moment of overlap, not from lack of ability but from a mind trained to distrust systems that would not expose their controls. Jace could faintly see the astral outline of Sophia and Nikos, both trying to pull from the other side.

Finally Jace said, "Stop trying to understand it. Just breathe and let us take you there."

Jace found the pulse. Sophia caught it. Together they pulled.

Nick arrived on the Astral couch curled around his stomach.

He looked once at the open structure of the room, once through the wall at Brandon's layered glow back in the Real, and once at his own hands.

"No," he said, beginning to heave.

Jace grabbed a bag from a table of supplies they had set up in the astral. Nick vommited in to it.

Sophia and Nikos patted him on the back, helped him clean his face and put his breathing mask on.

"How do you dispose of trash in the Astral?," Nikos asked.

Jace put gloves on, walked to the next room to the hull of the Victoria's, stuck his arm holding the bag through it, then let it go. It fell off into the void, slowly dwindling. Jace rubbed at his cold hands, turned and smiled.

Everyone stared at him. 

Nikos said, "You're right. It got weirder."

Nikos, armed and armored, had a notebook and was sketching and furiously writing notes in it. Grinning madly.

Nick sat on the couch with Cerberus pressed along one leg. He kept one hand in the dog's harness and his eyes on the deck. Which was hard because he could see through the deck into the station, see people in the distance that he'd known for years, see *inside* them, see *inside* everything. Everywhere. All at once.

He closed his eyes, which only partially helped. "I am staying here."

Sophia turned to Jace, "Shall we still do this with just the three of us?"

Jace finished putting on the armor and checking his butane torch, grabbed Sparky's remote and tested him. All lights green.

Sophia gave him a radio and placed two paper cards beside him. The first carried their planned route. The second Nick could write on and wrip pieces off to practice pushing from the Astral back to the Real. Nick took the first and ignored the second.

"Call me if you need me to come. Otherwise, I don't think my brain can do much other than try to watch you from here. And maybe puke again."

Jace said, "{Aura} wishes you luck. She asks if you can think of how to let her send analogue radio so she can talk on the radios to everyone. I guess the radios in my head are blocked from accessing the frequency we are using."

Nick smirked, realizing that Jace was giving him busy work to distract him, "I'll work on it."

Sophia sparked the igniter on her fuel valve.

A narrow blue flame appeared.

It leaned toward a direction that did not exist in the physical room.

"I dislike that," Nikos said.

"Useful things can be unpleasant," Sophia said.

"That is what Brandon says about the time you made wine."

She handed him the torch.

Jace took Sparky's remote. The drone still had no intelligence of its own on this side. Jace kept the thrust low and guided it by hand.

"Lets go hunt a snark."

##

The creature had left a trail.

In the astral, its burnt tissue appeared as dark curls inside the walls near the main stairway to Ring Three. Some marks followed surfaces. Others crossed empty projections where no physical corridor existed. Sparky recorded the trail in fragments. Jace saw it as a wound in the station's pale structure.

Nikos saw more.

"It is not moving through walls," he said.

"We watched it do that," Jace said.

"You watched one projection disappear into another. Look."

Nikos used his torch to point without touching. Three patches of burnt tissue appeared separated in Jace's view. Nikos shifted two steps sideways and all three joined into a single curve.

"It moved around the wall," he said. "Around a direction we cannot use."

"Stepping sideways," Sophia said.

Nikos gave her a sharp look.

"Now you say it as if it was always obvious."

They followed the curve, stepping through walls and around copper and silver pipes and patches. While they *could* go through those metals, it was uncomfortable and slow.

The trail descended into Ring Three and ended near the vacuum gap around the unlisted room. The room's silver skin remained a clean absence. Nothing beyond it resolved, not even the structural glow of the station. They moved slowly, lights off.

Sophia saw it first. The creature lay folded between that blind surface and the outer truss, like it wanted to get in but couldn't.

At first Jace mistook it for wreckage. Sparky's exhaust had opened a blackened trench across its back. Several limbs ended in hardened stumps. Its hide moved wetly around the damage, trying to close.

Nikos whispered into the radio, "That is alive?"

Jace went to shush him.

The red eye opened.

The creature unfolded.

Sophia struck first.

Her *push* caught the injured side and drove it toward the silver wall. The creature twisted around the push. Half of it moved one way, half another. It slipped past the impact and came at them across two surfaces at once. But, one of it's limbs hit the silver wall behind it and it jerked back in pain.

Jace stepped towards it and fired his torch, already set for maximum range of about a meter.

The flame caught one forelimb. Tissue bubbled and pulled away so quickly that the creature struck itself against the truss.

"What's happening?" Nick asked over the radio, hearing the squeals and their grunts.

"Fire works," Jace said.

"Do more of it," Nikos said, tension in his voice.

All three torches opened.

The creature folded behind a conduit. Jace's flame passed through the place where its chest had been. Nikos turned instead of following it, aiming into an empty angle above Sophia's shoulder.

The creature appeared there.

Nikos burned it across what would normally be called a mouth, but was nothing like one on earth-evolved creatures.

It screamed inside Jace's skull.

His vision broke into three valid images. In one, the creature climbed the truss. In another, it lunged at Nikos. In the third, it had disappeared.

{Aura} highlighted one that was somehow where all three overlapped.

"Nikos, get down."

Nikos dropped.

Jace flamed his torch at the highlighted place. The torch met resistance. Black fluid flashed into vapor, and the other two images collapsed into one thrashing mass.

"Now," she said.

Jace and Nikos closed from opposite sides.

They burned it.

The creature fought the force pinning it. One limb reached through the flame and wrapped around Jace's forearm. His armor smoked. The hooked tip found the seam beside his wrist and began to press through, freezing the skin. Jace yelped.

Nikos changed angle and burned the limb off Jace, making sure not to hit him with the flame. Nikos had surprisingly good aim.

Another limb struck Nikos across the chest. His ceramic plate cracked. He flew backward, hit the truss, and lost his torch.

Sophia *pushed* at it again and it fell over. She stuck her flame closer into it's central body.

The creature convulsed.

Its red eye divided into four smaller eyes, each looking in a different direction.

Its scream became a vibration, then pressure, then nothing.

The limbs released. The hide collapsed into layers thinner than paper. Those layers folded inward until no body remained, only a bright glowing smudge on the ground within the smouldering smoking floor plate.

It was the size of a plum and held more colors than Sparky's cameras could capture. Purple turned through amber. Fine green lines gathered at the surface and sank again. {Aura} zoomed in on it.

Jace shut off the torches.

The light from the monster pulled into it self, forming a sphere that moved toward Sophia.

"Do *not* touch it," Jace said.

"Its moving."

"That is not a reason to touch it."

The sphere reached her hand before she decided whether to withdraw it.

Light flowed across her fingers.

Sophia gasped.

The sphere emptied into her.

For a split second, she became brighter than the silver wall was dark. Gold and green moved through her arms and gathered behind her ribs. The space around her flexed. Nikos's fallen torch leapt from the deck and struck a conduit two meters away.

Sophia stared at her hand, at the torch.

"I didn't do that."

"You did something. That's like the one I touched on the *Falcon*," Jace said.

"Warm," she said. "It feels warm."

Nikos pushed himself upright, panting and seating and shaking from adrenaline.

"I would like to conduct the rest of this discussion inside the ship."

Jace retrieved Sparky and checked that the memory cards had recorded everything.

Nick's voice came over the radio, "Did we win?" 

They left the creature's absence beside the silver room.

##

Nick had been in the Astral for less than an hour.

He sat exactly where they had left him, one hand locked around Cerberus's harness. The red card lay ready under his other hand.

"That didn't sound as easy as you said the thing on the *Falcon* was to burn," he said.

"It was much much stronger. We killed it," Jace said.

Nick opened his eyes.

"Good. Get me out of here."

Sophia and Jace returned him first. Sophia going first, then pulling him. Cerberus crossed with them, pressed close enough to Nick that the dog's fur arrived tangled in Nick's fingers.

Nick became physical, rolled to one side, and vomited again. Cerberus jumped back then sniffed at the mess.

"That," Nick said when he could speak, "is not like anything in a game."

Brandon checked him while Jace and Sophia pushed Nikos across. Nick's {Aura} system on this side recorded each transition, power fluctuation, scale change, and physical symptom. Jace's {Aura} was recording from there, and they'd sync all the data together to see what they could learn.

##

It was late, but everyone wanted to watch the camera feeds. Nick had regained his composure, and had pulled out a large tablet that he'd brought with. It unfolded into screens and keyboards and joysticks. He was like a maestro, finally playing the instrument he excelled at. He helped the two {Aura}'s sync and pulled in the various camera feeds from sparky's data spike and the ones Brandon had set up locally.

Nikos was really getting into the retelling. He stopped the recording twice to explain where the creature had moved around a direction rather than through a wall. Sophia remained wrapped in a blanket, unusually warm, playing with her father's pen in both hands.

Brandon ignored the fight. He was upset that Nikos and Sophia and... well *everyone* were in danger. He was mad at Jace at how strong the creature had been, how much danger they all had been in. He was mad that aliens were invading. He was mad that science was broken.

Nikos was pointing, "This. This is what I mean. The stars are wrong. Look at Delta Cephei. Polaris. Just look!"

They all looked at him, then the screen. They had all spent years in space and were familiar, but the stars were there.

"{Aura} can you pull up the same star scape from outside Fortuna? Then overlay them."

The stars did not match.

"Look, see? Half the stars are off by centimeters! Which means light years as they are so far away. Is this a million years in the past? No, Polaris would be way over here. And these clouds? Look towards the inner system, it's like clouds of gas covering everywhere."

"Space is broken."
