# Chapter 23. Fire in the Walls

> *Athena: I cannot believe you, the AI hater, put an AI in your brain.*
>
> *Apollo: {Aura} says, "Stubborn humans. Conquer. Destroy."*
>
> *Athena: Your jokes are worse than Dad's. Still nothing from Artemis?*

By the following evening, the torches and armor were waiting in the Astral *Victoria*.

The first person Jace and Sophia tried to pull across was Nikos.

He lasted forty-three minutes before opening one eye.

"I am not there," he said.

"You are not supposed to talk," Sophia said, "Relax."

"I have been not talking for an hour. I am now reporting a failed experiment."

"You are halfway across," Jace said.

"That is worse."

Nikos closed his eye again.

They sat in the *Victoria's* observation room, suited and arranged in a circle on the floor. Jace held Nikos's left wrist. Sophia held his right. All three wore light pressure suits stripped of everything they could safely leave behind. Nikos had surrendered his tablet, belt hardware, watch, suit radio, and six sensor patches after Sophia explained how every additional circuit slowed the crossing.

Brandon watched from behind the open hatch with a physical camera on each wall and the expression of a man supervising surgery performed by people who had learned anatomy from social networking.

Nick sat beside him, narrating into the {Aura} on his wristcomp.

"His mass has dropped by eleven percent," Brandon said.

"Twelve," Nick said. "The floor scale averages too aggressively."

"I calibrated that scale."

"Then you calibrated it poorly, it should send updates faster."

Nikos opened his eye again.

"If I survive, I am separating you two."

Sophia squeezed his wrist.

"Quiet."

Jace felt Nikos as resistance rather than weight. Pulling himself across had become familiar, like finding a handhold in darkness. Pulling another person felt like trying to explain that handhold to every cell in a body that did not believe him.

He found the steady pattern inside Nikos and pushed sideways against it. Sophia pulled from somewhere Jace could not sense. For a few seconds, all three aligned.

The observation room lost depth.

Cold replaced warmth.

Nikos's hand became solid in Jace's grip.

The physical cameras saw him disappear.

In the Astral *Victoria*, Nikos opened both eyes and screamed once.

He stopped himself quickly, but not quickly enough to preserve his dignity.

"That," he said, breathing hard, "is not halfway into anything."

The observation room occupied more directions than it had in the Real. Its cabinets opened inward and sideways at the same time. The table showed every surface without turning. Brandon remained visible through the wall as overlapping color and structure, standing beside the place where Nikos's body no longer existed.

Nikos stared at him.

"He looks like a star collapsed into a nervous system."

"That description improves with time," Sophia said.

"No, it does not."

Cerberus stepped through beside Jace, shook himself, and sniffed Nikos's knee.

Nikos looked down at him.

"The dog is normal."

"Do not tell him that," Jace said. "He has worked hard on his reputation."

They left Nikos aboard the Astral *Victoria*, then returned to the Real with Cerberus before attempting Nick. Their own return took less time because both knew which direction to want. Cerberus followed without help, stepping between layers as easily as he crossed a doorway.

Nick had watched both transitions without making a joke. When Jace and Sophia became fully physical again, Nick removed his pearl earrings and set them in a padded case. Then he took off his overlay glasses.

Jace had never seen him without either.

"The earrings stay," Nick said. "The glasses stay. The gloves stay. I am not adding six hours because you want a technician on the wrong side of reality."

"You can stop," Jace said.

"I know. That is the only reason I have not."

Nick wore a simple mask, thermal layers, and almost nothing electronic. Jace and Sophia took his wrists. Cerberus settled against Nick's knee, ready to cross beside him.

Nick looked at Cerberus.

"If I vomit, I am blaming the dog."

Cerberus sneezed.

"Witnessed," Jace said.

Nick closed his eyes.

The second crossing took longer.

Nick fought it without moving. Jace felt him reject each moment of overlap, not from lack of ability but from a mind trained to distrust systems that would not expose their controls. Sophia whispered instructions in German, then French, then English. None helped.

Finally Jace said, "Stop trying to understand the interface."

"There is always an interface."

"This one is a tooth nerve."

Nick opened one eye.

"I hate that this makes sense."

He closed it again.

Jace found the pulse. Sophia caught it. Together they pulled.

Nick arrived on the Astral deck curled around his stomach.

He looked once at the open structure of the room, once through the wall at Brandon's layered glow, and once at his own hands.

"No," he said.

He vomited into the bag Jace had brought for exactly that purpose.

"That is a complete scientific statement," Nikos said.

Nick sat against the *Victoria's* inner hull with Cerberus pressed along one leg. He kept one hand in the dog's harness and his eyes on the deck.

"I am staying here."

"That was the plan," Jace said.

"It is now a law."

Sophia placed two paper cards beside him. The first carried their planned route. The second listed abort conditions. If the hunt team did not return within fifteen minutes, Nick would push the red card through the observation-room wall. Brandon would receive it in the physical tray and begin the return protocol. If Nick needed to leave sooner, Cerberus knew the way back to the crossing point, and Sophia had promised to return for him immediately.

Nick read the cards without lifting them.

"You built an emergency system out of stationery."

"It works on both sides," Jace said.

"That does not make it less stationery."

Nikos checked the three torches staged beside Sparky.

The team had spent most of the previous day moving them across one component at a time. They looked like cutting torches but carried separate butane and oxygen cartridges. Ambient gas in the Astral could not be trusted to support combustion. Jace had printed most of each body from aluminum and ceramic, then accepted the hours required to pull the valves and igniters through.

Nikos opened one fuel valve. Sophia sparked the igniter.

A narrow blue flame appeared.

It leaned toward a direction that did not exist in the physical room.

"I dislike that," Nikos said.

"Useful things can be unpleasant," Sophia said.

"That is what Brandon says about your wine."

She handed him the torch.

Jace took Sparky's remote. The drone still had no intelligence of its own on this side. Its three cameras recorded incompatible slices of the same volume, and its wheels sometimes chose different versions of the floor. Jace kept the thrust low and guided it by hand.

He looked at Nick.

Nick had closed his eyes. Cerberus remained against him, alert and unhappy.

"Bring back every memory card," Nick said. "I did not come here for you to lose the data."

"Not us?"

"The data is more obedient."

Jace, Sophia, and Nikos left the ship.

##

The creature had left a trail.

Its burnt tissue appeared as dark curls inside the walls between Ring Two and Ring Three. Some marks followed surfaces. Others crossed empty projections where no physical corridor existed. Sparky saw the trail in fragments. Jace saw it as a wound in the station's pale structure.

Nikos saw more.

"It is not moving through walls," he said.

"We watched it do that," Jace said.

"You watched one projection disappear into another. Look."

Nikos used his torch to point without touching. Three patches of burnt tissue appeared separated in Jace's view. Nikos shifted two steps sideways and all three joined into a single curve.

"It moved around the wall," he said. "Around a direction we usually cannot use."

"Stepping sideways," Sophia said.

Nikos gave her a sharp look.

"Now you say it as if it was always obvious."

They followed the curve.

The trail descended into Ring Three and ended near the vacuum gap around the unlisted room. Its silver skin remained a clean absence. Nothing beyond it resolved, not even the structural glow of the station.

The creature lay folded between that blind surface and the outer truss.

At first Jace mistook it for wreckage. Sparky's exhaust had opened a blackened trench across its back. Several limbs ended in hardened stumps. Its hide moved wetly around the damage, trying to close.

Nikos whispered, "That is alive?"

The red eye opened.

The creature unfolded.

Sophia struck first.

Her force caught the injured side and drove it toward the silver wall. The creature twisted around the push. Half of it moved one way, half another. It slipped past the impact and came at them across two surfaces at once.

Jace fired his torch.

The flame caught one forelimb. Tissue bubbled and pulled away so quickly that the creature struck itself against the truss.

"Fire works," he said.

"Do more of it," Nikos said.

All three torches opened.

The creature folded behind a conduit. Jace's flame passed through the place where its chest had been. Nikos turned instead of following it, aiming into an empty angle above Sophia's shoulder.

The creature appeared there.

Nikos burned it across the mouth.

It screamed inside Jace's skull.

His vision broke into three valid images. In one, the creature climbed the truss. In another, it lunged at Nikos. In the third, it had disappeared.

{Aura} did not choose.

Three outlines remained in Jace's display, each labeled with its source.

"Nikos, down."

Nikos dropped.

Jace burned the second outline. The torch met resistance. Black fluid flashed into vapor, and the other two images collapsed into the one thrashing above Nikos.

Sophia pushed again.

The creature struck the silver-clad room.

Every limb recoiled at once.

It twisted away from the wall with enough force to tear one burned leg free. The loose limb dissolved before it reached the deck. The creature came back toward Sophia, faster now, trapped between fire and something it feared more.

Jace drove Sparky beneath it and fired the remaining thruster.

The exhaust lifted the creature. Sophia caught it in the same instant and held it against the silver.

Her face went white.

"Now," she said.

Jace and Nikos closed from opposite sides.

They burned it.

The creature fought the force pinning it. One limb reached through the flame and wrapped around Jace's forearm. His armor smoked. The hooked tip found the seam beside his wrist and began to press through.

Nikos changed angle and burned the limb off Jace.

Another limb struck Nikos across the chest. His ceramic plate cracked. He flew backward, hit the truss, and lost his torch.

Sophia's hold wavered.

The creature peeled itself away from the silver.

Jace dropped Sparky's remote and caught Nikos's torch before it drifted out of reach. He opened both flames and crossed them through the wound Sparky had made the night before.

The creature convulsed.

Its red eye divided into four smaller eyes, each looking in a different direction.

Sophia raised her father's pen.

"Stay," she said.

The force returned hard enough to bend the silver panel inward.

Jace kept both torches inside the wound.

The creature burned from the center out.

Its scream became a vibration, then pressure, then nothing.

The limbs released. The hide collapsed into layers thinner than paper. Those layers folded inward until no body remained, only a sphere floating a handspan from the silver wall.

It was the size of a plum and held more colors than Jace's cameras could name. Purple turned through amber. Fine green lines gathered at the surface and sank again.

Sophia released her force.

Nikos slid down the truss, conscious but stunned.

Jace shut off the torches.

The sphere moved toward Sophia.

"Do not touch it," Jace said.

"It is moving."

"That is not a reason to touch it."

The sphere reached her hand before she decided whether to withdraw it.

Light flowed across her fingers.

Sophia gasped.

The sphere emptied into her.

For one second, she became brighter than the silver wall was dark. Gold and green moved through her arms and gathered behind her ribs. The space around her flexed. Nikos's fallen torch leapt from the deck and struck a conduit five meters away.

Sophia stared at her hand.

"I did not do that."

"You did something," Jace said.

"Warm," she said. "It feels warm."

Nikos pushed himself upright.

"I would like to conduct the rest of this discussion inside the ship."

Jace retrieved Sparky and every memory card.

They left the creature's absence beside the silver room.

##

Nick had been in the Astral for nineteen minutes.

He sat exactly where they had left him, one hand locked around Cerberus's harness. The red card lay ready under his other hand.

"You had sixty-one seconds," he said.

"We killed it," Jace said.

Nick opened his eyes.

"Good. Take me out of here."

Sophia and Jace returned him first. Cerberus crossed with them, pressed close enough to Nick that the dog's fur arrived tangled in Nick's fingers.

Nick became physical, rolled to one side, and vomited again.

"That," he said when he could speak, "is not like anything in the games."

Brandon checked him while Jace and Sophia went back for Nikos. {Misty} recorded each transition, power fluctuation, scale change, and physical symptom.

No one crossed again that night.

They watched the three camera feeds in the *Victoria's* galley instead. {Aura} preserved each view separately. Nikos stopped the recording twice to explain where the creature had moved around a direction rather than through a wall. Sophia remained wrapped in a blanket, unusually warm, her father's pen balanced across both hands.

Brandon ignored the fight.

He rewound six seconds of footage captured while Sparky faced away from Ring Three. The camera had looked out through Fortuna's incomplete outer structure and into the Astral sky.

He enlarged the frame.

"Where was the camera pointing?" he asked.

Jace checked the orientation log.

"Outward. About twenty degrees above the ring plane."

Brandon brought up Fortuna's physical navigation model and laid it beside the recording.

The stars did not match.

"Then either every camera turned without recording it," Brandon said, "or the sky is wrong."
