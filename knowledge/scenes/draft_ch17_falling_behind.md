# Chapter 17. Falling Behind

> *Most people do not know this, but in the Astral, movement works different that we expect, different than seems possible, and different than we planned for. Down is optional. Momentum is onvly sometimes optional.*
>
> Jace Grant, *unpublished notes*

Kim's message arrived at 0543.

*Do not report to Drone Ops today. Carlos and I are rolling back your routing model and checking every unit it touched. Stay home. Do not send a patch. Do not help remotely. We will talk tomorrow.*

Jace read it three times while Cerberus watched from the foot of the bed.

"That is... not a suspension," Jace said.

Cerberus tilted his head.

"It is a mandatory day off with unusually specific boundaries."

Cerberus yawned.

Jace opened his work queue. His permissions had already dropped to read-only. Sixty-three exceptions waited beneath his name. The red counter looked accusatory, which was unfair. Counters had no moral position.

He started a reply, deleted it, started another, and finally sent three words.

*Understood. I appologize.*

Kim did not answer.

Embarrassment arrived as heat under his collar. He had been on Fortuna for less than a month. He had already broken a ship, become famous in the shower, automated himself out of paying attention, and forced the most particular supervisor he had ever met to forbid him from helping.

On the other hand, he had a whole day free.

He could sleep. He could take Cerberus to the Barn. He could eat breakfast with Sophia before the rush and then spend an hour organizing the small physical workshop he had made aboard the *Victoria*. He could stay completely away from drones, routing, freight corridors, and anything Kim might reasonably describe as her problem.

It would not be so bad.

The public news display changed while he dressed.

Three missing-vessel icons became four.

The fourth ship had failed to report during a scheduled course correction near Saturn. No distress call. No debris. No drive plume. Its last cooperative navigation update showed every system green.

Beneath the ship report, another outer-belt station had missed two mandatory check-ins. Odysseus remained dark. A United States Department of Belt Security spokesperson appeared in a blue studio and said investigators now believed coordinated sabotage was the most likely explanation. The banner beneath her used larger words.

**U.S. DECLARES CHINESE SABOTAGE CAMPAIGN LIKELY, DEMANDS APPOLOGY**

The Chinese-language feed called the accusation preparation for an American seizure of belt assets. Mars security forces had moved ships toward two disputed transfer points. American contractors were reviewing Chinese station access. Chinese operators were doing the same to Americans. The Europeans were renegotiating contracts.

Two screens carried both versions side by side, seemingly from different multiverse worlds.

In the corridor outside his room, two Chinese construction workers stopped talking when Jace emerged. One looked at the Origin logo on his shirt, then at his face.

Jace looked down and zipped his jacket over the logo.

Cerberus leaned against his leg. Jace scratched behind his ears.

"Breakfast," he said. "A quiet, politically neutral breakfast."

##

The Barn was already awake.

Artificial morning rolled across the ceiling in pale bands. Goats complained from one pen. Chickens investigated the moral limits of their enclosure. A cow mooed in the background. Someone had opened the soil beds, and the massive room smelled like wet dirt, hay, animals, and a version of Earth assembled from memory.

Siren met them halfway down the path, tail flopping.

Cerberus accelerated. Siren stood still until the final second, stepped aside, and let him skid past her into a stack of feed sacks. Then she gingerly reached and bit his ear with affection.

Mei looked over from the duck enclosure.

"Early."

"Day off."

Her eyes narrowed. "Good off or bad off?"

"Administratively complex day off. Can he stay? I am going to see if Sophia wants breakfast."

Mei looked at Cerberus. Siren had him on his back. His tail hammered the floor.

"Yes. If he works."

Jace unclipped the leash and crouched beside Cerberus.

"I will be back soon. Do not let your sister recruit you into organized crime."

Siren sneezed in his face.

He left them playing beside the feed sacks and followed the curve of Ring Two toward the *Victoria*.

The station felt different from the day before. Nothing physical had changed. The shops were open. Cargo carts moved. The oak tree spread its leaves beneath a bright simulated sky. Yet conversations dropped when uniforms from different sections passed each other. Public displays showed maps of missing ships. An American security notice advised workers to report unusual Chinese network activity. A Chinese notice warned citizens not to surrender equipment for inspection without a representative from their leasehold.

Everyone had received the same facts and been given different enemies.

The *Victoria's* outer hatch opened for him.

"Good morning, Jace," {Misty} said. "Sophia is in her room."

"Perfect. Is she awake?"

"Her privacy settings do not permit passive biometric monitoring in personal quarters. She entered at 0118 and has not registered an exit."

"That is late, even for her."

"It is also early, depending on one's preferred framing. Brandon and Nikos are out at breakfast."

The corridor smelled of bread, herbs, and yesterday's coffee. Jace knocked on Sophia's door.

No answer.

He knocked again.

"Sophia? It is Jace. I have an unexpected day off and no breakfast plan."

Nothing.

"{Misty}, unlock the door."

"Sophia's privacy profile does not permit guest override."

"Emergency override."

"Please state the emergency."

Jace put his ear to the door. No movement. No shower. No muttered German.

"She does not answer. I'm worried about her, and if I am wrong she can yell at both of us."

The latch clicked.

Sophia's room was empty.

The blankets had been pulled aside. Her shoes rested beneath the bed. A breathing mask lay on the pillow, but the spare mask was gone. So was her thick coat.

On the desk, her father's wooden pen was missing.

Jace swore.

He called into the hallway, "{Misty}, when did she last communicate?"

"At 0116 she asked me to wake her at 0700. She entered her room two minutes later."

"No messages since?"

"None."

Jace closed the door. He pulled his own mask over his face and checked the seal. He switched to his crossing jacket, which was simple cloth with plastic buttons, a backup one he'd printed specifically to cross that had extra padding. The small crystal radio he had built waited in the drawer beside her bed. He clipped it to his collar, lay down where the physical bed overlapped the Astral room, and reached sideways.

The resistance caught every part of him at once.

He pulled.

Warm air became cold, blue, sharp. The physical *Victoria* thinned into layered walls and pale structure. His HUD vanished. The radio remained solid against his chest, wired to a speaker in his ear.

Sophia was not there.

The Astral workbench stood beneath its gear and labels. Pieces and gear waited in separate trays. Three emergency oxygen bottles rested against the wall. Sparky sat beside them with one green light glowing, camera mounted in his chasis.

Jace spoke over the radio. "Sophia?"

Static answered.

Then a shuddering breath.

"Jace?"

Her voice was so thin and tinny that he almost did not recognize it.

"I am here. Where are you?"

"I cannot... I can't get back. The station is moving. It keeps moving."

The next sound was a choked inhale.

"Slow down," Jace said. "Tell me what you can see."

"All of it. I can see all of Fortuna, but it is in front of me. I am outside. I fell out. The asteroid is under, I don't want to fall."

Jace looked through the *Victoria's* layered hull. Ring Two curved in both directions. The station remained exactly where it belonged.

"You cannot fall out of a station. You'd freeze to death, die from the vacuum."

"I am aware this should be true."

Her voice broke on the last word.

"How much air?"

"Twenty percent. Maybe less. The gauge froze."

Jace grabbed two emergency bottles and checked the physical needle. Full. He fastened one to Sparky's internal cargo clip.

"Listen to me. Do not move unless I tell you. Breathe in for four, out for six."

"Sheisse, I tried not moving. That is how it got farther away."

"Then breathe while moving. Four in. Six out."

He waited through one ragged cycle.

"Again."

The second breath was better.

"Good. Where did you leave the station?"

"Above Three. I saw something inside the wall. Tall like the robot you saw. It turned toward me. I stepped back, but back was through the outer hull. Then the wall was gone. The whole station slid away."

"Did it follow you?"

"I do not know. Jace, I am cold. I cannot feel my left hand."

He triggered Sparky and set the little robot to follow. Three thrusters lifted him unevenly from the deck.

"I am coming down to the launchpad. Keep talking."

"Down is not where I am."

"It is where the door is."

##

They had never gone this far through Fortuna.

Jace left the *Victoria*, crossed the transparent corridor of Ring Two, and entered the docking spine. Sparky flew beside him with the oxygen bottle knocking softly against his shell.

The familiar station became an immense crystal mechanism around them. Pressure walls nested inside support ribs. Cables descended in luminous bundles. Heat loops glowed faintly through decks that were not entirely solid. Living people burned as distant points inside the rotating rings. He realized he didn't have to walk all the way to a stair, he could just push himself through the floor. But, wary of also falling off, he decided not to experiment too much during an emergency.

At the central hub, spin gravity faded. Gravity was Mars-standard in the real, it was less here with gravity being more something you decided on, not that was decided upon you. Jace caught a guide rail that his hands could feel but his eyes could see through. He pulled himself toward the nonrotating shaft.

"Sophia, status."

Static.

"Sophia."

"Here."

"Air?"

"Very low. Can't see the percent, but the mask is slowing."

"You should see the station growing larger."

"I do not."

Jace pulled faster.

The shaft stretched through the station toward the asteroid. Physical elevators and cargo cages occupied parts of it, but in the Astral they became overlapping channels. Some were short. Others opened into blue distance and returned beside themselves. Jace followed the simplest path, hand over hand, while Sparky corrected around him in bursts.

Through the structure, Jace began to see very faint lines, like blurs where things were. It was like one of those old photos where you had to cross your eyes just right to see a shape.

The 'lines' trailed from every large piece of Fortuna. The rings carried broad, braided bands of pale light. The spine had a straighter one. Docked ships tied their thinner threads into the station's motion. Each person moving aboard made a brief, bright curve that joined the larger braid.

Fortuna was not only a place. It was a path, a braid, a vector.

The station orbited the Sun at tens of kilometers per second while rotating, translating, and making constant corrections. Physical matter shared that movement without having to choose it. In the Astral, the choice appeared to matter, was almost visible.

Sophia had stepped beyond the station's structure and separated from its 'path'.

She had not lost ordinary momentum. Ordinary momentum would have kept her beside Fortuna. She had lost something stranger: the station's claim that she belonged to its motion.

"I think I see what happened," Jace said.

"Please make the explanation short."

"The station has a line. A velocity line, maybe. Everything inside shares it. When you went through the outer wall, your line disentangled from ours."

"That is not physics."

"Nothing here fully respects physics. You are walking in space. Can you see bands of light near you?"

Silence.

"Yes, they are blurry. They look like dark roads that are too thin."

"The widest one should lead toward the station. Step onto it."

"There is nothing to step on. It's not a path."

"There is also no floor where you are standing, but you're walking. Try."

The radio carried her breathing and a small sound of effort.

"It moved."

"You moved?"

"I do not know. I stepped. Fortuna became closer, then farther."

"Keep following the widest line. Slow steps."

"Slow makes me stop."

"Then run."

Jace reached the bottom of the central *Fortuna* shaft. There was no gravity (or whatever passed for gravity here), so he moved hand over hand... then tried just stepping 'on' to space which somehow kind of worked. Sparky was in follow mode, thrusters requiring very little thrust, just aim.

The launchpad opened beneath Fortuna's central spine like a mechanical flower. Survey vessels, tugs, and cargo cradles look like translucent volumes. Beyond them was open space, blue-black and layered with faint streamers of motion.

Far behind the station, something small moved.

Sophia.

She ran with no surface beneath her feet. It was more surreal then every other surreal thing around him.

Each step landed on a pale band that became solid only when she touched it. Sometimes she paused and barely advanced. Then she would run again and her body *stretched* and blurred, crossing dozens of meters between one motion and the next. She snapped back into herself, stumbled, and continued running.

Fortuna continued around the Sun. Sophia chased it.

"I see you," Jace said.

She made a sound that might have been a laugh or a sob.

"I do not see you."

"I am at the launchpad. Up? Sparky is bringing oxygen."

Jace unbuttoned his jacket.

The cold struck immediately. It was freezing here, different parts of the station had different temperatures. The launchpad opened onto something worse. Cold gathered where the motion lines separated, as if heat itself needed a path to know where it belonged.

He wrapped his jacket around the oxygen bottle mounted inside sparky and stuffed in what would fit..

"Sparky, deliver to visual target. Human. That way. Go."

Sparky's green light blinked.

"Please do not miss."

The little robot crossed the launchpad boundary, straight through the floor.

For one second, he hovered beside Jace, just on the other side of a thick transparent multi-layerd wall. His trusters fired.

Then space pulled him *long*, he seemed to stretch as he rocketed off.

Sparky became a bright metal thread extending toward Sophia, contracted into a flaming ball, and appeared halfway to her. His thrusters fired at different rates, correcting for a geometry their controller had never modeled. The oxygen bottle swung beneath him. Jace realized that maybe explosive oxygen and thrusters should not be right next to each other and winced. Jace's jacket flapped in a wind that did not exist.

"Something is coming," Sophia said.

"It is Sparky. Catch him."

"He is moving too fast."

"He thinks the same about you."

Sparky slowed, stopped, and became two possible positions. Sophia lunged toward the nearest one. Her hand passed through an afterimage, then closed around the jacket. Jace, squinting could barely see them, falling into the distance.

"I have him."

"Bottle first."

Jace listened while she fought the frozen coupling. Her breath came in tiny, efficient sips. Metal struck metal.

Then the radio filled with the hiss of fresh air.

Sophia inhaled.

Jace sat down on the edge of the launch cradle because his legs stopped supporting him.

"Jacket," he said.

"Yes, mother."

Her voice remained weak, but it belonged to her again.

She pulled on the jacket. It hung loose over her coat. Sparky hovered beside her, one thruster firing in nervous taps. He had turned on a light, which helped Jace see them.

"Tell him to retrace the route," Jace said. "Return to sender."

"This makes no sense."

"Do your best."

Sophia and Sparky ran.

The robot rolled on nothing when his thrusters lost their reference. Sophia followed his green light. Sometimes both moved so slowly that Fortuna seemed to drag away from them. Sometimes their bodies elongated together and the distance collapsed in a single impossible stride.

Jace guided them by radio.

"Left. Your other left. Towards where we will be."

"There are six lefts."

"The one that makes me larger."

"They all make you wider."

"That is rude."

"I will die politely."

"You stopped dying when you got the bottle."

"Then I am freezing impolitely."

Sophia crossed the last stretch beneath the station.

She did not arrive at the launchpad. She arrived below it, separated from the nearest cradle by thirty meters of open Astral space. Sparky fired all three thrusters and rose. Sophia held his cargo clip.

The robot lifted her a few meter, but didn't have enough thrust to go higher.

Then both sank back onto the invisible road.

Sparky's light flashed amber.

"He cannot lift me," Sophia said.

"Let go. I will send him for a rope."

"How long?"

Jace looked behind him. The nearest physical emergency line was visible inside a sealed locker. Pulling it across would take time he could not count. The Astral lab had copper retrieval wire, but not thirty meters of load-rated rope. Maybe the construction zone?

"I will find one, it might take me a bit."

"Jace."

"Stay on the line. Sparky can bring the end down, just rest and follow the station."

"Jace, wait. There is something I did not tell you."

He looked at her below the station.

"This is a bad time for secrets."

"I know."

Sophia released Sparky.

She did not fall. One hand held the oxygen bottle. The other extended toward the launchpad.

Her outline brightened.

The pale motion lines around her bent.

Sophia *rose*.

Not like Sparky, with thrust and correction. She simply decided that the distance between herself and Fortuna should become smaller. Her body moved upward through open space. The station's broad velocity braid opened around her and took her back.

Sparky drifted away beneath her, falling off into the distance.

Sophia looked down, closed her fist, and pulled.

The robot rose after her. It's lights flashed in error.

His thrusters were off.

Jace forgot to breathe.

Sophia reached the launch cradle and caught his outstretched hand, pulling her through the floor. Her fingers were stiff and painfully cold. He pulled her into the structure. 

Sparky floated after her in jerks, and bumped gently against an outside gantry. Jace reached out *through* the wall, his arms cold (but not as murderously cold as he expected) and pulled the robot through the floor to their side.

For several seconds, Jace could only stare.

"You can fly."

"No."

"You just flew."

"It is not flying. I move things. Sometimes."

"You moved yourself. You moved Sparky."

"Yes."

"How long?"

She looked away.

"Before you found me."

"You have been hiding that?"

"I was trying to understand it. It does not behave like crossing. It is not in my equations. I wanted to see if you could, but you didn't. Sometimes it works when I am frightened. Sometimes it works when I am angry. Sometimes a cup moves when I do not want it to."

"That seems worth mentioning."

"I did not want you to look at me like that."

"Like what?"

"Like I am a witch."

Jace remembered the name she had given this place. Baba Yaga walking into the Never Never. A house larger inside than outside.

Sophia pulled his jacket tighter around her shoulders.

"When I was small, Baba Yaga was the thing in the forest that adults could not explain. She flew. She cursed people. Her house walked. I named this place after the story because the name made it less frightening. Then objects began moving when I looked at them. I did not want the story to be about me."

Jace picked up Sparky. The robot's casing was cold enough to hurt.

"You are not Baba Yaga."

"You have known me for eleven days."

"I have excellent instincts."

"You automated oxygen deliveries and stopped checking them."

"One of my instincts needs maintenance."

She laughed once, then shivered so hard that her teeth clicked.

"We need to get home," Jace said.

##

They climbed Fortuna's spine slowly, still hovering in the zero gravity.

Inside the station's structure when they crossed to the spinning wheels, Sophia's steps joined the broad movement lines again. The cold eased. Her hands began to hurt as feeling returned.

Jace kept one hand on her arm. Sparky hovered on the other side with the empty bottle clipped beneath him.

"Tell me what you saw," Jace said.

Sophia stared upward through the shaft.

"I was mapping the doubled corridors and went down to Three and was following where it got colder there. There was movement inside the wall. At first I thought it was a construction drone. Tall, narrow, folded to fit between spaces. Then it stood."

"Drones stand."

"It had too many joints."

"Some drones have many joints."

"It turned before I made a sound and had knives."

Jace stopped pulling along the rail.

"Did you see a face?"

"No. A shape. Two bright points. Red, perhaps. Then I stepped back too quickly and the station was gone."

"We do not go back to Three."

"You think it was one of those fighting robots you saw on the *Falcon*? Someone was hiding it here?"

Jace just shrugged, thinking.

They reached Ring Two. The *Victoria's* Astral hull settled around them, incomplete but stable. The workbench, labels, and half-built instruments looked almost domestic.

Sophia stood beside her physical bed and touched the place where she needed to return.

"Ready?" Jace asked. Her two colleagues had returned and were out in the galley talking.

"No."

"Good. Me neither."

They crossed together.

The physical room snapped into place. Warm air struck Jace's face. Gravity returned at an angle. Sophia collapsed onto the bed, still wearing his jacket over her coat and carrying the Astral oxygen bottle in the jacket pocket to keep the cold metal away from her fingers.

Jace sat beside her until the shaking slowed. He opened the door, brought her a coffee.

When he stepped out, {Misty} spoke from the ceiling.

"Sophia, your scheduled wake time passed two hours ago. Are you feeling ok?"

Sophia looked at Jace.

"Please record that I slept poorly and am taking a healing day, please go back into privacy mode."

"Sleep well you two."

Jace started laughing.

It came out too loudly. Sophia joined him, one hand over her eyes. They laughed until Jace's chest hurt and Sophia had to stop to breathe.

Then the wooden pen rose from where she had set it on her desk.

Jace stopped.

The pen floated between them in ordinary air.

No crossing. No blue cold. No transparent walls.

Sophia lowered her hand. The pen remained level.

"This is what I was afraid to show you," she said.

She turned her wrist. The pen rotated.

The ceramic cup beside it lifted next. A spoon rattled inside. Sophia's face tightened with concentration, and the cup drifted from the desk to Jace's open palm. She had gone from feezing to sweating.

It weighed exactly what a cup should weigh.

"You can do it here," he said.

"Not as well. Not for long."

The pen dropped. Jace caught the cup before it followed.

Sophia leaned back against the wall, exhausted.

"The crossing is not the magic," she said. "It is only where the magic was easiest to see."

They had never before used the word magic before. Outside the room, Fortuna continued its long fall around the Sun.

Inside, Jace held a cup that had flown.
