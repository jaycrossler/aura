# Chapter 22. Quiet Companion

> *Athena: She answered.*
>
> *Artemis: Sorry. Can't. Security drill. Second surge. Talk soon.*
>
> *Athena: What second surge?*
>
> *Apollo: There is something wrong with her messages. The packets skipped a lot of numbers.*
>
> *Athena: Complete messages usually contain the middle.*


{Misty} came back to full functionality eight hours after Jace touched her stack.

She remembered Sophia bringing Jace into the processor room. Her next intact memory began with Brandon holding a portable lamp over the open cabinet while Nikos read diagnostic codes from a paper checklist. Between those points lay fifty-three minutes of missing short-term storage, two damaged language indexes, and a power-state history that claimed she had been awake and asleep at the same time.

Lanchee performed the {Aura} installation in the Ring One clinic. The station's other physician, Doctor Yin, had refused because of Jace's injuries. Lanchee examined him, repaired two stitches, reapplied the bandages, and judged that he could tolerate the procedure. It was obvious she knew the wounds came from more than the bar fight, but she did not ask. She only gave Jace the same tired stare.

Nick had convinced her to supervise. Lanchee had assisted with several {Aura} installations, all without incident, but neither of them treated a firmware change inside Jace's HUD as routine.

Her observation room was the same one Jace had sat in aboard the *Falcon*, which now seemed like a year ago.

Lanchee narrated the settings and steps from memory.

"Full operating-system backup?"

"Complete," Nick said. "Offline copy in the secure clinic store. Second encrypted copy in his personal locker."

"Rollback test?"

"Passed."

"Local permissions?"

"Minimum set. No autonomous purchasing, no medical changes, no external data sharing. Read access to cameras and sensors. Hardware control requires separate approval by function."

Lanchee looked at Jace.

"A personalized system becomes more useful by observing you. You can revoke access. Revoking access may reduce function. Do you understand and accept responsibility for this change?"

"Yes."

"Say it without trying to finish faster."

Jace read the statement aloud and consented to each permission separately.

Nick placed the transfer patch behind Jace's ear. Cold spread along the implant seam.

The setup interface opened across his vision.

Three avatar options appeared: male, female, and a neutral geometric figure.

The geometric figure slowly rotated while displaying a percentage.

"That one looks like a pig butt," Jace said.

"I made it special for you," Nick said.

Jace selected the female avatar.

The figure resolved into a simple face without a fixed age or ethnicity. It did not smile. A voice spoke inside the private audio space formed by his implant.

{Aura}: *Initial review can include fourteen days of local sensor history, communications metadata, work patterns, and health telemetry. Content access remains disabled unless separately granted.*

"Metadata first," Jace said. "Ask before content."

{Aura}: *Confirmed.*

The review took six minutes. Warmth gathered beneath the skull cap as the local processors worked. Nick watched the temperature graph. Lanchee watched Jace, checking displays. She seemed satisfied and left to check other patients.

{Aura}: *Your recent behavior contains periods of high cognitive load, reduced sleep, elevated physical stress, and incomplete task closure. I recommend mostly quiet companion mode. I will answer direct questions, flag urgent safety conflicts, and defer nonurgent observations to a daily review. I do not want to overwhelm you.*

Jace glanced at Nick.

"You trained it to insult me."

"It met you ten minutes ago."

"Quiet companion mode," Jace said. "Daily review each morning."

{Aura}: *Confirmed.*

He opened the advanced settings.

Nick groaned.

"You just got it. Why does every engineer immediately mess with the settings?"

"I need raw camera, radio, and audio streams treated independently for reasons I will explain later. I want the model applied before normalization, with an independent clock for every sensor and hard labels for missing data."

"That is going to take more energy, so beware. I am still allowed to resent how quickly you found the correct menu."

They created local raw mirrors of the camera, HUD, suit telemetry, and Sparky feeds before Fortuna's cooperative processing layer normalized them. Operational controls remained connected. The separate record was slow and ugly but still ran well on Jace's ex-military HUD.

Jace loved it immediately. He really should have let Nick talk him into this earlier.

When they finished, Lanchee made him walk a straight line, read a paragraph, and identify four smells. Nick looked proud. Jace felt like a child with a new tool, transferring approved apps, data, and settings.

Sophia was still talking to her shipmates. A quick text from her read, *Going well. They did not believe the videos until I levitated them. They are reading papers now. Meet tomorrow with Nick?*

Jace went to his work shift late, which he had cleared with Kim. He spent the day using {Aura} to order prints, map corridors, and control drones. He stayed within his assigned permissions, but every task felt more intuitive than it had the day before.

The biggest change was the heads-up overlay. Limping through the halls and delivering packages, Jace saw who else had {Aura} systems in earrings, bracelets, or, more rarely, implanted HUDs. Names floated over people who had chosen to publish identifiers. Nearby systems exchanged public navigation notices, and Jace's minimap showed where a corridor was closed or a monitored door had been left open.

Nick had created something far more useful than Jace had appreciated. He hesitated, then granted {Aura} access to his personal archive while keeping medical records, work credentials, and external sharing disabled.

##

The next morning, {Aura} did not greet him.

It did not announce weather, play music, recommend breakfast, or ask how he had slept. It remained quiet while he fed Cerberus and flexed the last distortion out of his fingers. He ate with Hayes, who bounced Cerberus's ball and told Jace stories about famous bar fights from Fortuna's past. {Aura} made no comments or criticisms.

Jace approved.

At 0800, the daily review icon appeared.

He opened it, clicking mentally.

{Aura}: *As requested, I reviewed the logs from the environment you call the Astral and the papers your colleague Dr. Lotte provided. The evidence is inconsistent with a prank or ordinary sensor failure. It includes your HUD records and the independent files stored on your camera drone.*

Jace held his breath. Could {Aura} help him in the Astral? Could it work there? Nick's rollback package remained available if it could not.

"Please call her Sophia. The drone is named Sparky. I would like to put an {Aura} unit on him as soon as I get the chip from the Foundry."

{Aura}: *Subvocalization may improve privacy when you address me. The Astral records also contain structured distance discrepancies. If you permit it, I can propose experiments and prepare equipment recommendations for your approval.*

Jace groaned. {Aura} was as bad as Sophia in giving him more work to do.

{Aura}: *You also have unread messages. The chip ordered for Sparky is complete. {Alex} marked the design as obsolete and held it for quality review. Shall I prepare a confirmation that the order is intentional?*

"Yes. Show it to me before sending."

{Aura}: *Confirmed.*

Jace sent a quick voice message, *OK, Nick. I was wrong. {Aura} is awesome. If you still want to meet after work, I've got some things to show you. Meet at Sophia's?*

##

Nick hovered above the couch while a barking puppy jumped beneath him, trying to catch his shoes. Nick looked queasy. Sophia was flushed and breathing hard. Brandon looked as if he had finished an entire bottle of wine, his face red for a different reason. Nikos paced with a tablet, swiping angrily between paragraphs and diagrams.

Nikos's Greek accent grew heavier when he was passionate. "You cannot claim this is a separate dimension that you step into."

Jace cut in to preemptively defend Sophia. "I've been there. It's real."

Nikos raised both hands. "Of course it is real. I mean it may not be a separate universe. The evidence suggests that you remain in the same dimensional space but use some form of energy to move farther along a fourth spatial axis and back. Stepping sideways. It is very obvious."

Sophia and Jace looked at each other. It hadn't been obvious to them. Nick fell abruptly when Sophia stopped focusing, falling to the couch below him.

Nick finally said, "Why does it matter? I thought she was the physicist?"

Nikos turned, furious. "She is a physicist. I am an astrophysicist. It is very different. Are you merely a repairman, or are you an AI computational engineer?  It matters because if you merely *step further out* in four dimensional space, you don't have to invent whole new physics for *an entire new dimension*. You're just changing one thing, everything else is the same. You still breathe, you still live, there is still energy and mass."

Now, it was Jace and Nick's turn to look at each other.

Sophia broke the tension. "Okay. I acknowledge your point, I will fix the paper. Is there a way to measure the difference?"

Brandon interjected, before Nikos could answer. "And here. You show that copper and silver interfere with the visual effect. What about gold?"

"Um, why gold?"

Brandon looked almost as offended as Nikos. They truly were a matched pair.

"Because copper, silver, and gold share the same outer-electron pattern and occupy the same column of the periodic table. Why did no one test all three?"

Jace stood. He might not be the best Air Force offier in history, but he had sat thorough hundres of hours on how to run meetings and deal with opinionated scientists.

He intentionally made his tone respectful and complimentary. "Okay, okay. Thank you. This shows why we need your help. Sophia and I think we should have a shared experiment list to capture your ideas, an equipment plan, and rules for what happens next. Our first priority is the creature we wounded on Three. Tonight we start moving torches and armor. Tomorrow, after everyone understands the risks, we decide who crosses and who stays here to monitor."

He held up a butane-and-oxygen cutting torch and one of the ceramic-plated extreme-sport vests he had printed.

Nick raised his hand. "You want us to *go there*? First, what the hell? Second, this is crazy. Third, I am not sure I believe it. Fourth, I do not know how to 'step sideways.' Do either of you?"

He turned, and Brandon and Nikos just blinked.

Sophia stood. "We start with a demonstration. I will cross because Jace is injured and pretending otherwise. He can show you how we move equipment. We have a repeatable process, not an optimized one, and you may be able to improve it. We do not attempt to move another person tonight. Yes, Nikos. 'Step sideways'. Gott, I understand. Now, watch."

Sophia sat back onto the couch, became comfortable, and closed her eyes. Color gathered around her slowly. Nick stopped talking. Nikos stopped pacing. Brandon took a long drink of wine. They waited an uncomfortable amount.

Eleven minutes later, Sophia disappeared.

Thirty seconds after that, a small piece of paper appeared in front of Brandon's face with the word **Boo!** written on it.

Jace grinned. Everyone stared. Then began talking at once, speaking over each other. 

And then *there were five*.

Cerberus barked at the excitement. Six. Then there were *six*.
