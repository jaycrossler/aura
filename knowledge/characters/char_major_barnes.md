# Major Barnes

## Summary

Major Barnes was Jace's supervising officer in the drone-operations command that ran
the strike Jace refused. When Jace, reading his own drone take, judged that dropping a
series of bridges would kill an unacceptable number of civilians and retasked his fleet
elsewhere — sinking an expensive operation in the process — Barnes backed the call.
For six months that support held, and Jace believed the system had worked the way it was
supposed to: you do the right thing, and the right thing is protected.

Then the American Red party took power and purged the personnel it judged slow to follow
orders. Barnes is a mostly off-page figure, but he matters because his early backing is
what let Jace feel *right* rather than reckless — which makes the later betrayal by the
institution land harder.

## Role in the Bridge Incident

- Barnes received Jace's refusal and the retasking in real time.
- He did not overrule it. He supported it up the chain, on the record.
- His support is sincere and, by the standards that existed at the time, correct — this
  is not a man being cynical or covering himself. He believed Jace was right.
- The incident is **sealed**; Barnes is part of the record Jace is barred from discussing.
  He is therefore a name the reader may hear in fragments long before the full story.

## Characterization (light — he's a minor figure)

Steady, plain-spoken, unshowy. The kind of career officer who reads the take himself
before he signs anything. Not a rebel — a professional who thought the judgment call
was sound and said so. His decency is ordinary, not heroic, which is the point: the
purge did not need villains to do damage, only a new rule about who was expendable.

## Relationship to Jace

The last authority figure who backed Jace before everything came apart — and, quietly,
part of why Jace's grievance is grief rather than pure rage. Someone *did* stand up for
him. It just didn't matter in the end.
