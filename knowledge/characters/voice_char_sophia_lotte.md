# Character Voice Profile — Dr. Sophia Lotte

Sophia is the second-largest speaking role in Book 1 and the co-lead of the
Astral sequence from Ch13 onward. She is also the only character in the cast
who routinely changes language mid-sentence, which makes her the most expensive
role to produce on any of the local engines. Read the engine notes before
casting.

## The Accent Question

`char_sophia_lotte.md` and `visual_profile_sophia_lotte.md` both say: strong
German accent, French cultural heritage, code-switching where **French carries
emotion, German carries precision, English is neutral ground**, with the
Alsatian tic *"Hopla"* at the end of sentences when excited or flustered.

The Ch13 prose says her first line lands with *a slight French accent with
something else in it*, and the line itself is French.

Both are defensible for a Colmar-born, Strasbourg-raised woman with German
citizenship. They are not the same casting brief. **Flagged for author ruling.**
Until it is ruled, the safest production choice is the one that satisfies both
texts: a European-continental accent that a listener reads as French first and
German underneath, with the German surfacing specifically in technical
passages. That is a compromise, not canon.

## Vocal Characteristics

### Audio Profile
- **Voice type:** Clear mid-range female. Not low, not girlish. Carries.
- **Accent:** See above. Consistent, dry, and precise, never comedic. The
  visual profile is explicit on this point and it should be treated as binding.
- **Pitch:** Mid, with genuinely wide range. Unlike most of this cast she does
  use pitch expressively, and it is one of the fastest ways to tell her apart
  from Lanchee, Mei, and Suzi in a scene.
- **Speech rate:** Fast when excited, which is often. Slows dramatically and
  drops in pitch during what the visual profile calls `scientific-shock`.
- **Volume:** Moderate to high with friends, noticeably lower and cooler with
  strangers. The public and private registers should be audibly different.
- **Resonance:** Forward and bright when animated; flattens in notebook mode.
- **Distinctive features:** Emotional grunts and clicks used as punctuation.
  The visual profile instruction is to **hold them** rather than clip them.
  Trails off mid-sentence, writes the thought down, and resumes, sometimes ten
  minutes later. The resumption should sound like a continuation, not a restart.

**Casting note:** Warm, fast, animated, precise, and physically small. She is
5'2" and gestures hard enough that Jace has learned to lean back. Cast a voice
with real energy and real exactness in the same instrument. Avoid a
soft-spoken-scientist read; she is not soft-spoken, she is exacting.

### Vocal Variations by Emotional State

**Default (animated, mid-thought):**
- Fast, warm, self-interrupting, gestural. Sentences that turn a corner and
  arrive somewhere more precise than they started.

**Notebook mode:**
- Flatter, faster, very precise. Recording, not conversing. This is the register
  of the Ch13 epigraph. Read it as data, not as a confession.

**Scientific shock:**
- Voice drops and slows, almost reverent. The visual profile is explicit.
  Reserve this. It loses all power if used more than a few times per book.

**Cold with strangers:**
- Precise, professionally aloof, noticeably lower energy. Not hostile. This is
  the register a Fortuna acquaintance would describe her in.

**Warm with the group (Jace, Cerberus, Brandon, Nikos, {Misty}):**
- Spicy, teasing, fast. She jokes more than her reputation suggests. The Ch15
  "your place or mine" beat is her being deliberately funny and holding it for
  three seconds before laughing. She is not humorless and should never be cast
  as such.

**Isolation breaking (Ch13):**
- The laugh that breaks halfway through. Forty crossings of solitude come apart
  inside one sound. This is the single hardest line in the character and worth
  hand-tuning rather than generating.

**Barely-contained-relief:**
- Named mode in the visual profile. Sits between the warm and shock registers:
  fast like the warm mode, unsteady like the shock mode.

## Speech Patterns

### Verbal Tics and Habits
- *"Hopla"* at the end of statements. Frequency rises with excitement or fluster.
  Alsatian, meaning roughly "let's go," "okay," "sorry," or "right then."
- Corrects a preposition or a tense mid-sentence and moves on without apology.
- States the measurement before the conclusion.
- Argues by citing her own notebook count. *"I have six notebooks of evidence.
  You have one bad night and a dog."*

### Vocabulary Range
- **Technical:** Expert and unglossed. Dimensional physics, flux, compression
  relationships, instrumentation. She does not simplify unless asked.
- **Emotional:** More available than most of this cast, and it arrives in French.
- **Casual:** Warmer and more playful than her public reputation. Food, wine,
  Christmas, cheese, her father's pen.

### Relationship-Specific Speech

| With | Register |
|---|---|
| Jace | Increasingly the truest register. Collaborative, teasing, unguarded by Ch15. She does not adjust for his height and she does not soften her physics for him. |
| Cerberus | Delighted and undefended. *"He is very expressive. And a smart boy."* |
| Brandon and Nikos | Long-shared-service shorthand. They have given her space and not demanded an explanation, and that unspoken arrangement should be audible as care with a limit. |
| {Misty} | The only AI relationship in the book with real ease in it. Contrast deliberately with how Jace speaks to {Alex}. |
| Strangers on station | Cold, brief, professional. |

## Internal Monologue Style

Sophia is the POV character for the first half of Ch13, and a candidate
secondary POV later. Her narration is measurably different from Jace's: where
he diagnoses, she **observes and records**. Her interior voice numbers things,
notices what it cannot yet explain, and refuses to conclude early. The Ch13
opening ("She had not remembered to bring lunch") is the model: precise,
slightly wry about herself, and utterly unsentimental about her own suffering.

## Example Dialogue Anchors

- *"Mon Dieu. You are the delivery boy."* (Ch13, first words to another human in the Astral)
- *"He's real."* (Ch13, of Cerberus)
- *(epigraph, notebook mode, Ch13)* *"Pulling responds to intention but not to effort. I have confirmed this by producing a great deal of effort. If anyone reads these notes, stop treating it like a muscle."*
- *"One glass helps me relax. Not enough to impair me. Enough to make the transition easier."* (Ch15)
- *"I have six notebooks of evidence. You have one bad night and a dog."* (Ch15)
- *"I'm German, we are efficient. We drink 4 glasses per bottle."* (Ch15 — deadpan, and note it is one of the few places she names her own nationality)
- *"When we go back. Looks like two votes."* (Ch15)
- *"I want a room without station cameras. Mine has better air and fewer corporate microphones."* (Ch15 — the practical reason arriving after the joke)

## Speech-Affecting Conditions

- **Breathing mask, all crossings:** Muffling and audible breath. Routine, not
  an effect.
- **Cold:** Post-crossing at roughly minus twenty degrees she is wrapped in a
  blanket with Glühwein. Slight tightness and shiver in the voice is warranted
  here and almost nowhere else.
- **Exhaustion:** Chronic from before Ch13 and never fully resolved. Her
  crewmates and the station notice it. It should be a persistent low-level
  colour, not an event.
- **One glass of wine:** Canonically part of her crossing protocol. It should
  not be performed as impairment. It loosens her, nothing more.
- **Trauma aftermath, Arc 1 end:** Flinches at sudden loud noises. Relevant to
  later chapters, not to Ch13-16.

## Local TTS Engine Notes

Production guidance only, not canon. Verify against the model version installed.

### Orpheus
| Story tone | Orpheus rendering |
|---|---|
| default animated | No inline tags |
| the Ch13 broken laugh | `<laugh>` immediately followed by `<sniffle>`. This is the one place in her whole track where stacking two tags is correct, because the prose explicitly describes the laugh breaking into tears |
| notebook mode | No tags at all. Flat and fast |
| scientific shock | No tags. The drop is pace and pitch, and a `<gasp>` would cheapen it |
| Hopla | Never tagged. It is a word, not a vocalization |
| emotional grunts and clicks | These are **not** Orpheus tags and Orpheus cannot produce them from tags. They must be either performed in a reference clip or hand-inserted as audio. Flagged as a real production limitation |
| cold with strangers | No tags |

### Kokoro
- Kokoro's non-English packs cover French (`ff_siwis`) and the base American and
  British female packs cover the neutral English. There is **no German pack** in
  the standard voice set, which matters given the accent question above.
- Practical approach: generate her English dialogue from an English female pack
  and her French interjections from `ff_siwis`, then splice. This is laborious
  but it is the only way Kokoro will pronounce *"Mon Dieu"*, *"Hopla"*, and
  *"Glühwein"* correctly.
- Alternatively, accept a single English voice throughout and pre-write the
  French phrases phonetically in a lexicon. Cheaper, and loses the code-switch.
- Kokoro cannot produce the emotional grunts and clicks. Accept their loss or
  splice them.

### Chatterbox
- **Best fit of the three engines for this character**, because reference-audio
  cloning is the only realistic way to get a consistent European accent with
  French-forward emotion and German-forward precision from one voice.
- Reference clip brief, 15 to 25 seconds: a European woman in her early thirties
  explaining an experimental result in accented English, fast, precise,
  enthusiastic, interrupting herself once.
- `exaggeration` moderate to high. She is the most expressive character in the
  cast and the setting that would over-drive Jace is roughly right for her.
- Consider a **second reference clip** for notebook mode, flatter and faster,
  and switch clips per passage rather than trying to get both from one setting.

### All engines
Sophia and Lanchee are the two precise, clinical, non-American female voices in
the book, and they appear in the same rooms from Ch19 onward. Audition them
back to back before locking either. If they blur, move Sophia brighter and
faster rather than moving Lanchee.
