# Twenty Future Epigraph Candidates

These are working candidates rather than assigned chapter epigraphs. The historical
quotations use short, source-checked wording. The sibling exchanges and Sophia papers
are original material written for the manuscript.

## Historical scientists and engineers

### 1. Isaac Newton

> “If I have seen farther, it is by standing on the shoulders of giants.”
>
> Isaac Newton, letter to Robert Hooke

Best fit: a chapter where the team turns earlier observations into a usable model.

Source: [The Newton Project](https://www.newtonproject.ox.ac.uk/view/texts/normalized/OTHE00101)

### 2. Richard Feynman

> “What I cannot create, I do not understand.”
>
> Richard Feynman

Best fit: a camera, scanner, drone, or rocket-building chapter.

Source: [Caltech](https://magazine.caltech.edu/post/biology-through-the-eyes-of-a-physicist)

### 3. Ada Lovelace

> “The Analytical Engine has no pretensions whatever to originate anything.”
>
> Ada Lovelace

Best fit: an {Aura} chapter, particularly when a human mistakes an AI inference for
mere obedience.

Source: [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/blogs/taking-measure/ada-lovelace-worlds-first-computer-programmer-who-predicted-artificial)

### 4. Katherine Johnson

> “Everything is physics and math.”
>
> Katherine Johnson

Best fit: a navigation, ranging, or orbital-mechanics chapter.

Source: [NASA](https://www.nasa.gov/learning-resources/katherine-johnson-a-lifetime-of-stem/)

### 5. Margaret Hamilton

> “There was no choice but to be pioneers; no time to be beginners.”
>
> Margaret Hamilton

Best fit: the first operational use of equipment the team has barely learned to trust.

Source: [MIT News](https://news.mit.edu/2009/apollo-vign-0717)

### 6. Alan Turing

> “We can only see a short distance ahead, but we can see plenty there that needs to be done.”
>
> Alan Turing

Best fit: a chapter where the group accepts an incomplete theory and proceeds carefully.

Source: [The Turing Digital Archive](https://turingarchive.kings.cam.ac.uk/node/2)

## Apollo, Artemis, and Athena

### 7. Accurate nouns

> *Athena: You cannot end a message with “space is broken.”*
>
> *Apollo: I ran out of accurate nouns.*
>
> *Artemis: Start with whether you are alive.*

Best fit: immediately after a discovery that Jace cannot safely explain.

### 8. The organized sibling

> *Apollo: I met a physicist who keeps seventeen notebooks.*
>
> *Athena: So you finally found the organized sibling.*
>
> *Artemis: That was never in doubt.*

Best fit: a Sophia-centered chapter or the first major consolidation of her notes.

### 9. Units

> *Athena: How far away are you?*
>
> *Apollo: Thirty-seven light-minutes.*
>
> *Athena: That is a unit of time.*
>
> *Artemis: He is having a difficult week.*

Best fit: a communications-delay or distance-compression chapter.

### 10. Safety requirements

> *Artemis: Do not test anything you cannot turn off.*
>
> *Apollo: That eliminates most science.*
>
> *Athena: And all siblings.*

Best fit: before an experiment that becomes more energetic than planned.

### 11. The Pig Channel

> *Athena: Mom watched the Pig Channel clip six times.*
>
> *Apollo: Cerberus did the difficult part.*
>
> *Artemis: That has never stopped you from taking credit before.*

Best fit: a lower-tension chapter that returns briefly to family and Jace’s public reputation.

### 12. Naming a dimension

> *Apollo: I need a shorter name for a place that has too many directions.*
>
> *Athena: Upstairs?*
>
> *Artemis: Do not let Athena name a dimension.*

Best fit: the formal transition from Never-Never to Astral, or a later naming dispute.

### 13. The stars

> *Athena: Are the stars the same from there?*
>
> *Apollo: I thought so.*
>
> *Artemis: That answer got worse while I was reading it.*

Best fit: a chapter that returns to the wrong-sky problem or expands its implications.

## Selections from Sophia Lotte’s papers

### 14. Atmosphere

> *Field Note 42: Ambient temperature remains near minus twenty Celsius. Detectable
> oxygen remains below a useful breathing threshold. “Survivable” is not the same as
> “habitable.”*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Never-Never Notebook Three*

Best fit: environmental engineering, suits, scrubbers, or the first plant.

### 15. Cameras

> *Three calibrated cameras produced three incompatible images. Calibration did not
> fail. The assumption that one image must be correct did.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Projection Experiments, Revision Six*

Best fit: imaging, medical scans, or {Sparky} model development.

### 16. Communication

> *Radio does not cross. Motion does. This is not communication until noise can be
> distinguished from meaning.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Interface Notes*

Best fit: the single-rod relay, three-rod telegraph, or sixteen-rod array.

### 17. Metals

> *Copper impairs visibility. Silver blocks it more completely. Cause unknown. Two
> observations are not a law, even when one of them saves your life.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Material Interference Table*

Best fit: later metal testing or a return to the silver-lined room.

### 18. Living crossings

> *Living subjects do not cross like cargo. Resistance changes with attention, fear,
> and cooperation. Mass alone does not predict transfer time.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Assisted Crossing Protocol, Draft Two*

Best fit: expanding the crossing team or formalizing medical controls.

### 19. Distance

> *Four becomes two. Nine becomes three. Sixteen becomes four. The arithmetic is
> elementary. The location where it is true is not.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Preliminary Astral Ranging Results*

Best fit: the first distance-law chapter or a later propulsion consequence.

### 20. The plant

> *The saxifrage remains alive after thirty-one hours. This proves that the root
> insulation works. It proves nothing about long-term habitability.*
>
> Sophia Lotte, *Field Station Biology Log*

Best fit: a chapter showing the Astral room becoming an inhabited research station.
