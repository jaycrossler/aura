# Audiobook Narration Prose Style

Use these rules for chapter drafts and scene prose.

1. Favor short sentences and compact paragraphs. Vary sentence length enough to keep the narration from sounding mechanical.
2. Create pauses with periods, commas, semicolons, and paragraph breaks.
3. Do not use em dashes or spaced dashes as routine pause marks. Reserve hyphens for ordinary compound words.
4. Keep action easy to follow when heard once. Name the actor when a pronoun could become ambiguous in audio.
5. Keep dialogue tags light, but include them when a change of speaker might not register by voice alone.
6. Put technical detail inside action, decisions, consequences, or character observation. Avoid lecture blocks.
7. End scenes and chapters on a changed fact, a difficult choice, a sharp image, or an unanswered question.
8. Preserve project typography. AI system names use curly braces. Named abilities do not use square brackets in prose until Jace establishes that convention in-story.
9. Prefer specific sensory details over stacked adjectives. Use one strong detail, then move.
10. Read dialogue and action aloud during revision. Replace punctuation that asks a narrator to infer an unnatural pause.
11. In chapter manuscripts, spell the terms as `AI` and `AIs`. In audiobook exports, convert both singular and plural forms to `A.I.` so text-to-speech systems pronounce the letters separately.
